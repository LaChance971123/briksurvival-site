Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Write the plan

    Record who calls whom, the agreed lawful channel, check-in windows and the backup meeting or contact method. Keep a paper copy with each radio.

  2. 02

    Test actual locations

    Try normal indoor and outdoor positions, distances and terrain with authorized users. Mark dead spots without putting anyone in danger to improve reception.

  3. 03

    Use short messages

    Identify yourself and recipient, give location or status, then ask for confirmation. Pause so others can respond and avoid talking over a reply.

  4. 04

    Plan a failed check-in

    Use the agreed retry and backup method. A missed call may mean battery, terrain or timing problems; it is not automatically proof of an emergency.

Receiving tones are filters

CTCSS/DCS settings can suppress audio from some other users, but they do not encrypt a conversation or reserve a channel. Others may hear you and the channel may still be occupied. Match settings for compatibility and avoid sharing sensitive medical, financial or security information over an ordinary open radio link.

Power and range routine

Keep batteries maintained according to the device instructions and have a suitable charging plan. Terrain, buildings, antenna position, interference and batteries affect coverage. Repeaters may fail or be inaccessible. An alternative check-in method is more reliable than a promise that one radio will always reach everyone.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Follow the agreed backup meeting and contact plan. Do not leave a safe location or climb an exposed structure merely to find signal. Keep the household informed of any safe route change.

People & practical needs.

Use names and everyday words rather than complex codes. Practice with children and less technical adults in safe conditions; assign responsibility for charging and returning each radio.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep the equipment and written information described above where the responsible person can reach them. Test access in darkness, with a tired household, and while carrying the items you actually need.

Next steps.

Run a short lawful check-in drill, deliberately skip one call, and verify that everyone follows the agreed backup action.

Common questions.

Is buying equipment enough?

No. Check that it fits the intended task, can be used by the people who need it, and has a maintenance plan. Practice in safe conditions before relying on it.

Watch for.

Only transmit where the service rules and equipment permit. Do not treat open-channel communications as confidential.
