Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Choose a short review

    Set a recurring household check suited to your schedule. Start with one kit or room rather than unpacking every item at once.

  2. 02

    Inspect and rotate

    Review food labels, water arrangements, batteries and equipment according to their instructions. Replace used items and record damaged or missing supplies.

  3. 03

    Confirm the people

    Check contact numbers, destination permission, transport arrangements and household changes. A plan can fail because a helper moved, even when all the gear still works.

  4. 04

    Practice one action

    Find a light in darkness, locate the contact card or pack the carry bag. Keep drills calm and safe; record friction rather than scoring people.

A four-part cycle

Editorial rotation: one month review contacts and destinations; next review food and water; next review lighting, charging and radios; next review carry bags and mobility needs. Add checks after using supplies, moving home or changing care arrangements. This is a suggested rhythm, not a replacement for the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule.

Close the loop on problems

Write three fields: problem, owner and next action. A dead flashlight should produce a specific task such as replacing its suitable batteries and testing it, not a vague note to buy more equipment. Keep the list short enough that the household can finish it before the next review.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

When services are already disrupted, avoid lengthy audits. Check immediate essentials and critical care first, then use a short task list to keep track of what needs replenishing or repair.

People & practical needs.

Let each person practice the part they may need to do. Adapt checks for mobility, vision, hearing and anxiety. Do not use surprise drills, smoke or simulated violence to make a household exercise feel realistic.

Keep essentials within reach

A simple inventory, a current contact card, product instructions and a small list of unresolved tasks.

Next steps.

Choose a ten-minute check this week. Find one missing item, one outdated contact or one awkward access problem and fix it.

Common questions.

How often should everything be checked?

Use product instructions for equipment-specific intervals. Add a manageable household review rhythm and revisit the plan after changes or use; a monthly check is an organizational suggestion, not a technical standard.

Watch for.

Do not test emergency devices by creating a hazard. Follow specific alarm, battery, medical-device and radio instructions rather than inventing a universal test procedure.
