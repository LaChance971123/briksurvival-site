Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Download before the outage

    Use the map app’s offline feature for the whole journey, not just the destination. Test it with mobile data off.

  2. 02

    Keep a paper backup

    Write addresses, landmarks, route choices and emergency contacts. Carry a paper map if available.

  3. 03

    Check route suitability

    Consider flood zones, damaged structures, access needs and transport restrictions. The fastest app route is not necessarily the safest.

  4. 04

    Conserve device power

    Reduce unnecessary screen use and keep a power bank available. Know how to view the downloaded map without needing a new connection.

  5. 05

    Reassess as conditions change

    Stop in a safe place to compare the route with visible conditions and recent trusted reports. Do not enter an uncertain road simply because the map displays it.

Check the route from both ends

Study the approach to the destination as well as the route out. Record recognizable junctions, alternate access and where the household will meet if a pickup point becomes unreachable. Test downloaded map coverage before a trip rather than assuming the overview map includes every street. Keep written addresses separate from the phone; avoid posting a private destination in a public message.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use known routes and written landmarks while checking conditions from safe positions. If you cannot identify a safe onward route, reassess rather than consuming the last battery on repeated rerouting.

People & practical needs.

Assign a trusted adult to each person who needs assistance. Keep medicines, essential contacts and a workable reunion plan with the group. Make decisions around the least mobile person, not the fastest; avoid splitting the family without a clear, safe way to reconnect. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: download before the outage. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens.
