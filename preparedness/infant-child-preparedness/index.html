Infants & children during disruption

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Keep children with a trusted caregiver and plan safe feeding, water, hygiene, medicines and warmth around their age and needs. Put written contacts with each child and agree a reunion plan. Use age-appropriate feeding guidance; do not dilute formula or use unsafe water to stretch supplies.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Name the caregiver

    Decide who stays with each child during sheltering and travel. Use a familiar trusted backup; do not hand children to unverified helpers.

  2. 02

    Protect feeding

    Maintain breastfeeding where applicable. Ready-to-feed formula can avoid preparation water needs when available; follow product and age-specific guidance. Do not improvise formula or change dilution.

  3. 03

    Keep hygiene supplies

    Pack nappies, wipes, soap, clean clothing and safe water for essential care. Keep feeding equipment clean and separate from waste.

  4. 04

    Keep a contact card

    Use necessary caregiver contact details and medical information without displaying sensitive identity details publicly. Teach older children the agreed safe contacts.

  5. 05

    Use simple explanations

    Explain the next action calmly and specifically. Familiar routines and a comfort item can help; avoid promising that you know when danger will end.

Build around one ordinary day of care

List what each child uses in an ordinary day: feeding, drinking, hygiene, medicines, communication, sleep and safe transport. Then identify which tasks depend on clean water, electricity, refrigeration, a particular adult or a school. Infants, toddlers and teenagers need different plans. Do not apply an adult pantry or water calculation as a complete infant-care plan.

Write the information so the backup adult can use it. Keep product names and care instructions specific where needed, and separate private records from a simple contact card. Review quantities as the child grows rather than leaving an old diaper bag labelled emergency-ready indefinitely.

Protect the feeding method instead of improvising substitutes

CDC identifies ready-to-feed infant formula as the safest formula option during emergencies because it does not need mixing water. If formula is used, check suitable products, supply and storage with the child’s needs in mind. Follow label instructions after opening and feeding; unopened shelf stability does not make an opened container safe indefinitely. Do not dilute formula, make homemade replacements or change prescribed feeding without qualified advice.

Powdered formula needs a separate safe plan

It requires appropriate safe water, correct preparation and clean feeding equipment. If it is the only available option, use CDC’s specific guidance and seek qualified feeding support. A general camping water treatment instruction is not an infant-feeding protocol.

If breastfeeding or expressing milk, plan access to appropriate support and any necessary equipment, cleaning and storage. A powered pump introduces dependencies that direct feeding may not have; discuss a workable backup with a qualified lactation provider. Do not assume stress automatically stops milk production or that an unfamiliar feeding technique can safely be learned from a hurried improvisation.

Reference: CDC · Infant feeding during emergencies

Separate feeding, diapering and clean storage

Organize care supplies by clean and soiled tasks. Keep the feeding area and equipment separate from diaper waste, cleaning chemicals and contaminated belongings. Use safe water and appropriate cleaning instructions for reusable feeding items. A packet of wipes does not replace every cleaning step required by a bottle or pump. If the required cleaning cannot be done, seek a suitable supported alternative rather than repeatedly reusing contaminated equipment.

  • Keep age-appropriate feeding supplies and their instructions together, with a plan for opened items.
  • Place diapers, suitable personal-care items, clean clothing and waste containment in a separate clearly marked group.
  • Use a dedicated clean storage arrangement for prepared feeding equipment.
  • Record where suitable handwashing water and soap are available.
  • Keep product instructions and clinician-directed medicine information readable; do not estimate doses from an adult product.

Editorial example: a family packs enough formula but forgets the equipment and water needed to clean its reusable bottles. The food quantity alone does not support feeding. Walk through one complete feed and one change in normal conditions to identify the missing task. Keep normal care available throughout this exercise.

Reference: CDC · Infant and young-child supply checklist

Keep a trusted caregiver and a clear handover

Assign who stays with each child during sheltering, departure and arrival. A child should not be left alone while an adult searches for supplies or signal. Keep a trusted backup and current school or childcare pickup permissions. Record the expected destination and how another household adult learns it. Avoid two collectors arriving independently while nobody maintains the contact plan.

Teach an older child the agreed trusted contacts and an age-appropriate separation response without placing adult responsibility on them. Keep sensitive identity and medical details off visible public labels. A child with communication or sensory needs may need a particular format, familiar aid, or calm explanation. Tell the backup caregiver what actually works instead of describing the child only by a diagnosis.

Reference: American Red Cross · Household emergency planning

Plan safe arrival, rest and a manageable routine

Before relocation, ask what the destination can support for the child: safe feeding, suitable sleeping arrangements, essential care, hygiene, temperature and a caregiver. Do not assume an evacuation site has infant supplies merely because families can enter. Keep an appropriate transport arrangement and follow the child-restraint or carrier instructions; emergency travel is not a reason to improvise unsafe restraint.

Use simple truthful explanations: what is happening now, where the trusted adult is, and the next step. A familiar story, quiet activity or comfort object may help preserve routine. Avoid promises about when danger ends or repeated exposure to frightening media. Ask qualified child-health support about concerning illness or changes rather than treating this planning guide as a diagnostic checklist.

Test quantities and identify the first care gap

Inventory supplies against actual use and review them when needs change. For a child using a particular formula or prescribed item, count only suitable usable stock. Keep a daily-use grab list for items that cannot remain in the bag. Check expiration, storage and preparation instructions, and replace supplies used during normal life. A large bag with the wrong sizes or products is not an adequate reserve.

  1. Walk through a normal care period with the backup adult.
  2. Identify water, power, refrigeration and cleaning dependencies.
  3. Check the carry load while accounting for who holds or assists the child.
  4. Confirm the destination’s care arrangements and pickup permissions.
  5. Write the first essential gap and the safe action to resolve it.
  6. Recheck the plan after growth, a care change, travel or supply use.

If disruption already prevents safe feeding or necessary care, seek verified qualified assistance promptly while keeping the child with a trusted caregiver. Do not stretch supplies through unsafe dilution or borrow someone else’s treatment instructions. Use a suitable alternative location if available and confirmed. The purpose of the reserve is to preserve care and time to act, not to justify waiting through a worsening situation.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use the caregiver plan and safe supplies already available while seeking verified medical or feeding support. Do not stretch formula with extra water or leave a child alone to find supplies.

People & practical needs.

Name the caregiver and trusted backup for each child. Preserve safe feeding and care, keep children supervised during supply searches, and make communication and handovers appropriate to age and individual needs.

Keep essentials within reach

Child-specific feeding and hygiene supplies, suitable clothing, prescribed care items and instructions, trusted caregiver contacts, authorized collection details, appropriate transport equipment and a manageable comfort item.

Next steps.

Plan quantities for the actual feeding and care routine, and ask a qualified clinician for special dietary or medical needs. Include medications and transport or sleeping arrangements.

Common questions.

Can I dilute formula to make it last longer?

No. Follow the product and qualified care instructions. If safe supplies or preparation are unavailable, seek verified feeding support; do not improvise dilution or homemade replacements.

Is a well-stocked adult kit enough for an infant?

No. Feeding, cleaning, storage, hygiene, transport, sleep and caregiver arrangements need their own child-specific plan and usable supplies.

Watch for.

Infants can deteriorate quickly with dehydration, temperature exposure or illness. Seek qualified care for concerning symptoms; do not rely on adult treatment advice.
