Emergency documents: a private, usable recovery folder

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Organize the information needed to identify yourself, contact providers and recover after disruption. Keep protected copies in separate places and check that you can actually open them.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Gather the essentials

    Identify relevant identity papers, insurance details, housing records, medical information and financial contacts. Prioritize what your household would need away from home.

  2. 02

    Make a contact index

    Record provider names, official phone numbers and enough account information to locate the service. Keep full sensitive records separate from an everyday contact card.

  3. 03

    Protect copies

    Store copies separately from originals and protect digital access. Consider fire, water, loss and unauthorized viewing when choosing storage.

  4. 04

    Test retrieval

    Check that a trusted authorized person can find the information if you are unavailable. Test access without your usual phone and note any account recovery dependence.

Separate three levels of information

Editorial organization: an everyday card with limited contacts; a travel folder with relevant copies; and a protected archive with fuller financial and identity records. This reduces the need to carry every sensitive document everywhere. Label folders plainly so a tired household can find housing, care and insurance details quickly.

Treat passwords differently

Avoid putting account passwords beside a complete identity and financial file that could be lost. Use a secure access arrangement suited to your household, and document recovery steps without exposing secrets. A password-protected file is not useful if its only unlock information is on the phone you just lost.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If online services fail, use the paper contact index and protected copies for available in-person or phone services. Do not give documents to an unverified person claiming to arrange aid.

People & practical needs.

Include each household member’s relevant records and discuss permission to share them. Plan language support, accessibility and how a caregiver can locate necessary care information.

Keep essentials within reach

A limited contact card and protected travel copies where you can retrieve them quickly; a separate backup in a location unlikely to be affected by the same event.

Next steps.

Choose five records you would miss most after losing access to home. Check their dates, make protected copies and test retrieval.

Common questions.

Should everything go in my go bag?

Carry what you realistically need and protect it. A bag can be lost or stolen, so keep a separate backup and avoid carrying a single unprotected collection of all your sensitive records.

Watch for.

Do not post identity documents, account numbers or recovery credentials in public help requests. Storage and legal requirements vary; consult relevant providers about acceptable copies.
