Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Identify the exact radio

    Record model, regulatory identification and manual edition. Similar-looking radios can be intended for different services and markets.

  2. 02

    Separate receiving from transmitting

    A frequency you can enter is not automatically one you may transmit on. Check the relevant national radio service rules, licensing and device certification.

  3. 03

    Learn safe controls

    Use the exact manual to learn power, volume, channel selection, keypad lock and receive settings. Avoid transmit testing until you have confirmed permission and a suitable frequency or channel.

  4. 04

    Make a practical communication plan

    Choose compatible, lawful equipment with the people you need to reach. Test it in normal conditions at the locations you will actually use.

U.S. service distinctions

FRS uses certified FRS equipment. GMRS requires appropriate certified equipment and applicable licensing. A generic programmable amateur handheld does not become a certified FRS or GMRS radio by entering those frequencies. Amateur radio has its own operator and station requirements; learn them through current official guidance and qualified local instruction. Rules outside the U.S. differ.

Receiving alerts has limits

A handheld that receives a weather broadcast may not provide reliable automatic alerts or SAME location-specific functions. Check the exact manual. Keep phone alerts and an appropriate weather radio as separate options. A charged radio is useful only when you know what to monitor and whether the desired service is available at your location.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use verified receive-only functions and your written contact plan while working out licensing or equipment questions. Do not assume that a crisis creates unrestricted permission to transmit on any frequency.

People & practical needs.

Label the agreed channels and controls in plain language. Choose a radio that less technical household members can operate, not only one that an enthusiast can program.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep the equipment and written information described above where the responsible person can reach them. Test access in darkness, with a tired household, and while carrying the items you actually need.

Next steps.

Find your exact manual and certification information, then write down the service, authorized users and a lawful routine test plan.

Common questions.

Is buying equipment enough?

No. Check that it fits the intended task, can be used by the people who need it, and has a maintenance plan. Practice in safe conditions before relying on it.

Watch for.

Do not transmit on public-safety, aviation or other unauthorized channels. This is an equipment and planning overview, not legal authorization or programming instructions for a specific model.
