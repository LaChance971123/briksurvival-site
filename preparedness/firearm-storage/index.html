Secure firearm storage and emergency relocation

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Secure unloaded firearms with a storage system suited to the household, and lock ammunition separately. Plan how lawful storage will continue during evacuation, visitors or displacement.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Assess who could gain access

    Include children, guests, contractors, unauthorized adults and people at risk of self-harm. A hidden drawer is not secure storage.

  2. 02

    Choose and install a storage system

    Compare lockboxes, safes and locking devices appropriate to the firearm and manufacturer’s instructions. Consider authorized access, resistance to removal and whether installation is permitted.

  3. 03

    Control keys and codes

    Keep keys inaccessible to unauthorized people. Do not share combinations casually or store them on the cabinet. Check who knows the code after household changes.

  4. 04

    Plan relocation

    Verify transport rules, destination restrictions and available locked storage before travel. Secure cases against unauthorized access; avoid leaving firearms in unattended vehicles.

Match storage to real conditions

Check fit, locks and mounting with equipment made safe according to its manual and competent supervision. A device that prevents firing may not prevent theft; a case suitable for transport may not replace installed storage. Follow both product and firearm instructions rather than improvising a locking arrangement.

Water, heat and damaged storage

A wet safe, broken lock or displaced cabinet can create access and reliability problems. Keep people away from unsecured firearms. Ask a qualified professional about a lawful storage and inspection plan; do not assume that drying a flooded firearm makes it usable. Document damage without unnecessary handling.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Keep unauthorized people away from the area and use an existing suitable locked container if safely possible. If you cannot establish secure storage, seek a lawful alternative before moving others into that space.

People & practical needs.

Check access for caregivers and visitors, not only residents. Include a responsible adult’s contact information in the relocation plan without advertising where firearms are stored.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep the equipment and written information described above where the responsible person can reach them. Test access in darkness, with a tired household, and while carrying the items you actually need.

Next steps.

Review locks, mounting, keys and destination arrangements at each seasonal household check.

Common questions.

Is buying equipment enough?

No. Check that it fits the intended task, can be used by the people who need it, and has a maintenance plan. Practice in safe conditions before relying on it.

Watch for.

Never compromise escape routes or use storage arrangements that create entrapment. Storage laws and transport rules vary by location.
