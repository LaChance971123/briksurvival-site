Count needs before containers

Plan at least one gallon per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation, with additional water for heat, pregnancy, illness and other needs. Add pets and any equipment requirements. A three-day supply is a starting point; expand within safe space and budget rather than promising that a fixed amount covers every household.

Keep a rotation record

Use suitable food-grade containers and follow CDC cleaning and filling guidance. Label filled containers and avoid heat, direct sunlight and chemicals. Replace self-filled stored water every six months; follow commercially bottled water labeling. Practice pouring without contaminating the container or sharing a drinking utensil.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Calculate the household quantity on paper and store smaller containers if lifting a large container is difficult. Keep one portable supply ready. Divide storage between safe places so damage to one container does not lose all the household water.

People & practical needs.

Build the plan around the least mobile person and the shortest critical supply. Try carrying the kit, opening containers and using the light in the dark. Spend first on safe water, essential medicines, basic food and communication rather than specialised gear you cannot use. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: calculate needs. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Do not reuse containers that held toxic substances, even after washing.

Keep essentials within reach

For emergency water storage, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: Calculate the household quantity on paper and store smaller containers if lifting a large container is difficult. Keep one portable supply ready. Divide storage between safe places so damage to one container does not lose all the household water. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

First decision: Calculate needs

Count people and days and add water for pets and extra needs. Decide which person can carry this out immediately and what would prevent it in your current location. Do not delay the protective step to complete a household worksheet.

When the original plan fails

Calculate the household quantity on paper and store smaller containers if lifting a large container is difficult. Keep one portable supply ready. Divide storage between safe places so damage to one container does not lose all the household water. Reassess the next available location or resource using the warning signs in this guide; do not assume a promised service is operating.

Before changing location

Do not reuse containers that held toxic substances, even after washing. Check the actual route and the needs of the person who will have the most difficulty moving. A destination is not confirmed until access and conditions have been checked.

Questions in the field.

What matters before collecting supplies?

Store at least one gallon of water per person per day for three days; aim for two weeks if practical. Allow more for heat, illness, pregnancy and pets. Use commercially bottled water or clean food-grade containers, and keep it cool and away from chemicals. Collect items only if doing so does not delay this action.

What should another household member do?

Use a second person to handle communication or dependents while the first carries out the immediate action. For emergency water storage, keep the specific hazard in view: Do not reuse containers that held toxic substances, even after washing.

What should be written down?

Record when the situation changed, what action was taken and what remains uncertain. For this situation, start with: Rotate supplies. Follow bottled-water dates and replace self-filled stored water every six months. Keep personal notes private; they are not submitted to Osprey Zero.
