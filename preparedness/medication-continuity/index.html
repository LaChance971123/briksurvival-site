Medicines during disrupted services

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Keep essential medicines, a written medication list and the supplies needed to administer them accessible. Follow each product’s storage instructions and protect medicines from floodwater, freezing and excess heat. Contact a pharmacist or clinician for a product-specific shortage or storage problem; do not change doses or substitute medicines on your own.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Write the essential list

    Record the medicine name, strength, usual dose, prescriber, pharmacy and allergies. Keep a paper copy with the medicine and a copy with a trusted contact.

  2. 02

    Count usable supply

    Track what remains and identify which medicine interruption is most urgent. Include inhalers, needles, testing supplies and batteries, not just tablets.

  3. 03

    Protect storage

    Use the product label and pharmacist advice for temperature limits. Avoid direct heat and sunlight; do not let temperature-sensitive medicines freeze against ice packs.

  4. 04

    Contact alternatives early

    Try a reachable pharmacy, clinician or verified medical aid provider before the supply is exhausted. Ask about the exact product; replacement rules vary.

  5. 05

    Keep treatment unchanged unless advised

    Do not ration by skipping or doubling doses without professional direction. Seek urgent medical help for deterioration rather than trying an unverified substitute.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use the written list to seek qualified advice through a reachable pharmacy or verified clinic. Preserve safe storage and track supply. If a life-critical treatment cannot be maintained, prioritise relocation to care before the last dose or battery is used.

People & practical needs.

Write names, strengths, schedules, allergies, prescriber and pharmacy contacts and any storage needs. Keep that information private and accessible to a trusted caregiver. Include supplies needed to administer the medicine, not only the package itself.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Before a disruption, agree a refill and storage plan with the prescriber or pharmacist, within local rules. If you use insulin or powered equipment, keep its specific emergency instructions with the kit.

Common questions.

Can a warm medicine still be used?

Storage limits depend on the exact product and whether it has been opened. Record the name, time and temperature if known and contact a pharmacist or clinician. Do not apply a generic insulin rule to every medicine or throw away an essential supply without checking safe replacement options.

What if the usual pharmacy is closed?

Keep the current prescription and medication list ready and seek a reachable licensed pharmacy or care team. Verify the source before accepting donated medicine. Do not change doses, share prescriptions or use unverified substitutes to extend supply.

Watch for.

A medicine can be unsafe after heat or flood exposure even when its package looks normal. Product-specific advice matters; there is no universal safe unrefrigerated storage period.
