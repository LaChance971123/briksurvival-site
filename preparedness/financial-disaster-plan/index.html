Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Build a contact list

    Record verified contacts for housing, utilities, insurance, banking and essential care. Use numbers from account statements or the provider’s own site.

  2. 02

    Protect important records

    Keep identity, policy and account information in protected storage with a separate backup. Decide who can access it if you are unavailable.

  3. 03

    Understand your insurance

    Check covered hazards, exclusions, deductibles and claim instructions with your insurer. Standard home insurance generally does not include flood or earthquake damage.

  4. 04

    Prioritize essential needs

    Editorial planning: list the costs of safe housing, transport, food, water and care. Choose a realistic saving target after essentials; avoid buying gear with money already needed for bills.

A worksheet you can build yourself

Editorial fields: provider, verified contact, policy or account reference stored privately, regular due date, usual payment method and alternate contact method. Keep a separate expense list for relocation and recovery. Update contact details after changing providers so an old document does not lead you to the wrong service.

Practice finding the records

Choose one adult to simulate a lost-phone situation. Can they find the insurance contact and housing reference without your phone, email inbox or password reset? Resolve the access problem safely, then return the documents to protected storage. The exercise should not expose passwords or account numbers to the group.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If providers cannot be reached, keep dated notes of attempted contact and unavoidable expenses, preserve receipts and try verified channels when service returns. Do not assume a payment pause or assistance approval exists until confirmed.

People & practical needs.

Plan for a person who normally manages the finances becoming unavailable. Give an appropriate trusted person the access they actually need, while keeping sensitive records away from children and public shared folders.

Keep essentials within reach

Protected records, verified contacts, essential-cost list and a way to retain receipts. Do not carry every original document if that creates an unnecessary loss risk.

Next steps.

Check one policy and one backup-access method this week. Ask the insurer specific coverage questions rather than assuming every storm loss is covered.

The policy wording is unclear?

Ask the insurer and retain the response.

An aid message asks for sensitive data?

Verify its source before sharing anything.

Common questions.

Is there a single correct emergency savings amount?

No. Use your actual household costs and constraints. A small achievable reserve and usable records are practical starting points.

Watch for.

Anyone promising guaranteed aid or demanding unusual payment may be a scam. Verify through independent official channels before providing identity or account information.
