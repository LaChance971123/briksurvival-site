Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Pick one scenario

    Choose departure, a communications failure or locating equipment in low light. State the objective and stop conditions before starting.

  2. 02

    Protect essential care

    Do not disconnect necessary medical equipment, withhold medicines or create hazardous heat, cold, smoke or fire. Tell participants it is a drill.

  3. 03

    Use real equipment

    Try the actual bags, chargers, contacts and accessible routes. Note missing adapters, forgotten keys, unclear roles and uncomfortable carrying loads.

  4. 04

    Review and fix

    Ask what worked, what failed and one concrete change. Assign an owner and a date, then repeat only the affected part.

A simple monthly rotation

Rotate a short contact check, supply-date review, departure rehearsal and safe equipment familiarization session. Keep the schedule manageable so it survives normal life. Record an action completed, not just a readiness score. A checklist full of untested assumptions does not establish capability.

Include failure deliberately

Try a unavailable driver, a phone with no signal or a forgotten charger in a controlled setting. Ask which alternative is usable without help. Do not simulate hazards by actually disabling safety systems or blocking exits. A drill should make the household calmer and more capable, not frightened or exhausted.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Practice the protective steps you can complete independently. Keep a short paper plan so the exercise does not require internet, a paid app or a responding service to succeed.

People & practical needs.

Adapt drills to age, disability, language and anxiety. Use predictable steps, consent and breaks. Record where help is needed rather than labeling someone as the problem.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep the equipment and written information described above where the responsible person can reach them. Test access in darkness, with a tired household, and while carrying the items you actually need.

Next steps.

Choose one ten-minute drill this week and complete its single highest-value improvement before scheduling the next.

Common questions.

Is buying equipment enough?

No. Check that it fits the intended task, can be used by the people who need it, and has a maintenance plan. Practice in safe conditions before relying on it.

Watch for.

Never stage surprise armed scenarios, real smoke exposure, dangerous driving or deprivation exercises.
