Preparedness on a limited budget

Do now.

  1. 01

    Inventory before buying

    Check usable water containers, lights, batteries, food, clothing and charging options. Record quantities, expiry dates and gaps.

  2. 02

    Choose one realistic priority

    Match the first purchase to a likely disruption and a person’s essential need. A medicine or water gap matters more than specialist gadgets.

  3. 03

    Build into normal routines

    Add a small amount of shelf-stable food or water when affordable. Rotate what the household actually uses and can prepare safely.

  4. 04

    Make free planning changes

    Write emergency contacts, choose meeting places, save guides and learn safe shutoffs. Practice an exit route and check warning access.

Spend against a specific gap

Start with actions that cost little: update contacts, learn exits, inventory medicines, test chargers and choose destinations. Next, buy the missing essential with the greatest practical benefit. Ordinary food you already eat, appropriate water containers and reliable lighting often beat a box of unfamiliar gadgets.

Count continuing costs

Include replacement batteries, expiry, storage space, training and subscription fees. Avoid equipment that requires consumables you cannot afford to replace. Record what each purchase does and who can operate it; a low price is not good value if the item cannot support the intended task.

Buy the missing capability

Before buying, name the problem the purchase will solve: seeing in darkness, carrying safe water, opening food or contacting the household. Check whether you already own a suitable item. Budget for any required batteries, cable, storage or maintenance as part of the same purchase. A sale price is not a saving if the equipment cannot be used by the person who needs it.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Plan around actual carrying capacity, storage, transport and money. Identify safe mutual support before a crisis, but confirm what a neighbor or group can provide. A borrowed item is only useful when it will be accessible during the disruption.

People & practical needs.

Ask each person what they need to function without power or transport. Include sensory access, infant feeding, pets and medical routines. Keep a written priority list so shared household purchases solve a real problem rather than duplicating equipment.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep the action checklist and written contact details accessible. Use the equipment named in this guide only when available and appropriate. Assign someone to check access, batteries and expiry dates before an incident; retrieving a kit must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Revisit one gap at a time. Check a kit when food or medication rotates, after household changes and before the local hazard season.

Little storage space

Choose compact supplies that are useful in daily life, keep exits clear and identify a safe storage location.

No purchase is affordable now

Complete contacts, escape planning, guide saving and a household inventory first. Ask a trusted local support group about verified resources when safe.

Common questions.

Do I need a large commercial kit?

No. Start with practical needs and equipment the household can use. Compare an actual inventory with the likely disruption before buying.

Watch for.

Do not buy unsafe used safety equipment, store fuel improperly or ration essential medication to build a kit. No shopping list guarantees survival.
