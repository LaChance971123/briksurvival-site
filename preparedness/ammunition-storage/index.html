Ammunition: compatibility, storage and inspection

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Use only ammunition confirmed for the exact firearm by its markings and manufacturer’s manual. Keep ammunition locked, dry, identifiable and away from unauthorized people; isolate uncertain or damaged rounds from use.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Keep labels intact

    Retain original packaging and identifying information. Do not mix loose cartridges or identify ammunition by appearance alone.

  2. 02

    Confirm compatibility

    Match the manual, firearm markings and ammunition packaging. Similar names or dimensions do not establish that a cartridge is safe for a firearm. Ask the manufacturer or a qualified professional if uncertain.

  3. 03

    Protect storage

    Use secured storage separate from unloaded firearms. Follow manufacturer guidance on moisture, heat and storage; keep oils, solvents and water away from ammunition.

  4. 04

    Stop using damaged or uncertain ammunition

    Do not fire corroded, deformed, flooded, contaminated or unidentified ammunition. Arrange lawful disposal through qualified local channels; do not burn, dismantle or throw it into a fire.

A simple inventory

Record caliber exactly as labeled, manufacturer, lot when available, quantity and storage location in a private record. Inventory supports compatibility and accountability; it should not be posted publicly or attached to an easily shared household contact card. Review condition when checking storage, without unnecessarily manipulating cartridges.

After flooding or household disruption

Keep suspect ammunition separate from normal stock and restrict access. Do not try to restore wet ammunition by heating it. Disposal services and transport requirements differ; describe what you have and ask for instructions before taking suspect material to a facility.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If reliable identification or inspection is unavailable, do not use the ammunition. Securing it and postponing use is safer than guessing from an online comparison photo.

People & practical needs.

Keep keys, packaging and loose cartridges away from children and visitors. Include ammunition security in household relocation planning without giving untrained people responsibility for handling it.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep the equipment and written information described above where the responsible person can reach them. Test access in darkness, with a tired household, and while carrying the items you actually need.

Next steps.

Audit labels and storage access. Mark anything uncertain as not for use and arrange appropriate professional advice.

Common questions.

Is buying equipment enough?

No. Check that it fits the intended task, can be used by the people who need it, and has a maintenance plan. Practice in safe conditions before relying on it.

Watch for.

This guide does not provide reloading, ammunition manufacture, modification or performance optimization instructions.
