72-hour emergency kit

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Start with at least three days of safe water, ready-to-eat food, essential medicines, lighting and communication supplies. Include one gallon of water per person per day as a starting point, and account for pets, heat, medical needs and accessibility.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Inventory what you own

    Gather usable supplies before buying duplicates.

  2. 02

    Cover essentials

    Choose safe water, shelf-stable food, a can opener, light, radio, batteries and phone charging.

  3. 03

    Personalize

    Add medication arrangements, eyeglasses, infant needs, pet supplies, hygiene and accessible equipment.

  4. 04

    Maintain the kit

    Label dates, rotate food and water as instructed and check supplies routinely.

Separate storage from portability

A home reserve can include larger containers and comfort supplies; a departure bag must be carryable. Do not assume the whole home kit can leave with you. Label where essential portable items are stored and who carries them.

Maintain by household changes

Review medicines, clothing sizes, pet needs, care equipment, contacts and transport alongside dates on food and batteries. Test cables and lights. Three days is an initial planning target, not a guarantee that services will return by then.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If shops close today, inventory water, shelf-stable food, medicine and batteries already at home. Put them together and write down gaps. Build a usable kit in stages; a partly stocked kit is more useful than waiting for an expensive perfect one.

People & practical needs.

Start with the people in the home: drinking water, medicines, infant feeding, sensory access, safe light and pet care. Choose who will carry shared items. A 72-hour kit is a planning starting point, not a guarantee that a disruption ends after three days.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Build longer-duration supplies as space and budget allow. Keep an evacuation bag separate enough to carry.

Common questions.

How much can the household carry?

Separate supplies stored at home from a bag intended for evacuation. Test carrying the bag with the actual caregiver and mobility needs. Heavy water and duplicate tools can make a complete-looking kit unusable when movement matters.

What if a supply expires?

Use normal household rotation where suitable and record refill dates. Check battery and device compatibility before relying on them. Medicine quantities and replacements require a clinician or pharmacist rather than an improvised rationing plan.

Watch for.

A standard checklist is only a starting point; three days may not cover a prolonged disruption.
