Food when cooking is unavailable

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Use safe ready-to-eat food, protect drinking water and keep hands and utensils clean. Do not run a camping stove, grill or charcoal indoors. Check food-storage times and temperatures after power loss; never taste suspect food to decide whether it is safe. Plan around dietary and medical needs.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Inventory safe food

    Count shelf-stable items that can be eaten without heating and identify a manual opener. Separate suspect refrigerated food from safe supplies.

  2. 02

    Plan small portions

    Open only what can be eaten safely when refrigeration is unavailable. Read the label for storage after opening.

  3. 03

    Protect water and hygiene

    Use safe water for food and essential washing. Keep preparation surfaces separate from toilet waste and dirty containers.

  4. 04

    Keep combustion outside

    Use cooking equipment only in the location and manner its instructions allow. Never burn charcoal or use a camp stove inside a room, garage or enclosed balcony.

  5. 05

    Include special needs

    Plan for infants, allergies, diabetes, swallowing difficulty and pets. Do not change prescribed diets or infant formula preparation without qualified advice.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use known safe shelf-stable food already present and a written meal inventory. If no safe cooking position exists, choose food that does not need cooking rather than improvising an indoor flame.

People & practical needs.

Assign portions around actual dietary needs and available safe drinking water. A manual opener should be usable by the person preparing food. Keep pet food and special feeding supplies included rather than assuming a generic emergency meal works for everyone.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Replenish through a verified reachable source before supplies become critical. Set aside easy-to-carry food for movement and avoid creating a plan that depends entirely on fuel or refrigeration.

Common questions.

Can everyone eat the stored food?

Check allergies, chewing or swallowing limitations, infant feeding and the need for safe water. Prefer foods the household already eats. A no-cook food still needs safe storage and handling after opening.

What if there is no safe refrigeration?

Choose shelf-stable portions that can be used without keeping unsafe leftovers. Keep opened food away from heat, insects and contamination. Do not make a meal plan depend on a working refrigerator when power is uncertain.

Watch for.

Food that looks and smells normal can still be unsafe. Do not salvage leaking, swollen or flood-contaminated packaging.
