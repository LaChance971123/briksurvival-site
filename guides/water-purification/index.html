Water purification

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Use safe bottled water when available. For microbial risk, bring clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet, and cool before use. Water contaminated with fuel, chemicals or radioactive material cannot be made safe by boiling or ordinary disinfection.

Build your plan.

  1. 01

    Check the source

    Avoid floodwater or sources suspected of toxic contamination; find an approved alternative.

  2. 02

    Clarify cloudy water

    Let sediment settle or strain through a clean cloth before treatment; this alone does not disinfect it.

  3. 03

    Boil for germs

    Use the appropriate time and cool safely in a clean container.

  4. 04

    If boiling is unavailable

    Use a treatment product as labeled and official guidance for your water source; filters have different capabilities.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Choose the least contaminated available source and establish whether the problem is microbial or chemical. If the source may contain fuel, industrial chemicals or radioactive material, find another source; a camping filter or boiling is not a universal solution.

People & practical needs.

Keep untreated and treated water in different labeled containers. Use clean utensils and teach each person how safe water is dispensed. Plan extra care for infant feeding and people with weakened immunity; a field filter is not automatically suitable for every medical use.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Store treated water in clean covered containers and prevent recontamination.

Common questions.

Does clear water mean safe water?

No. Clarity does not show whether germs, fuel, pesticides or other chemicals are present. Select the safest source available and identify suspected contamination before choosing treatment. Boiling or ordinary filtration does not resolve every chemical hazard.

What if the label differs from an online recipe?

Follow the exact treatment product instructions for the water condition, volume and contact time. Do not mix products or estimate a stronger dose. A device’s microbial claims and maintenance requirements must match the actual task.

Watch for.

Clear water can still contain germs. Ordinary filters may not remove viruses or chemicals.
