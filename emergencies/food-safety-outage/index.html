Food safety after power loss

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed. Without power, a refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours and a half-full freezer about 24 hours. Never taste food to test safety. Use official discard guidance and a thermometer.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Note the time

    Record when power failed and avoid opening cold storage.

  2. 02

    Use temperatures

    Check appliance and food temperatures when safe; appearance and smell do not prove safety.

  3. 03

    Discard risky food

    After 4 hours without power or another cold source, discard refrigerated perishables such as meat, eggs and leftovers.

  4. 04

    Check frozen food

    Food with ice crystals or at 40°F or below can generally be refrozen or cooked; use official food-specific charts.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Track the time power stopped and use a fridge thermometer if available. If time or temperature is unknown, discard suspect perishable food and use shelf-stable food. Spend scarce fuel on safe water and essential cooking rather than trying to rescue unsafe food.

People & practical needs.

Keep children and pets out of damaged and contaminated areas. Use a clean area for water, food and sleeping while repairs are assessed. Record losses only when it is safe, and do not trade a stable temporary shelter for a hazardous early return.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep essential medicines, drinking water, a battery light, shoes, phone power and a written contact card within reach. Adapt the load to the hazard and the people with you; do not delay escape to collect equipment.

Next steps.

Clean appliances and replace spoiled supplies. Keep thermometers available before the next outage.

Watch for.

Do not serve questionable food to people or pets.
