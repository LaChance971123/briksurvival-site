Do now.

  1. 01

    Check the original

    Find the actual organisation or person through an independently known channel. A familiar-looking account or copied badge can be fake.

  2. 02

    Check place and time

    Confirm the affected street, region and date. Old footage and information from another country can appear current when forwarded.

  3. 03

    Compare independent information

    Use radio, direct trusted observations and verified humanitarian or official sources as appropriate. Several reposts of the same claim are not independent confirmation.

  4. 04

    Separate facts from guesses

    Write what is confirmed and what remains uncertain. Do not treat a claimed ceasefire, route, water safety notice or aid offer as certain without recent verification.

  5. 05

    Share carefully

    Forward only necessary verified information with its time and source. Protect live locations and personal information. Correct earlier messages if they prove wrong.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If connectivity fails, use the observations you can obtain safely and your written plans. Do not travel into danger to confirm a rumour or follow a single anonymous message.

People & practical needs.

Assign a trusted adult to each person who needs assistance. Keep medicines, essential contacts and a workable reunion plan with the group. Make decisions around the least mobile person, not the fastest; avoid splitting the family without a clear, safe way to reconnect. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: check the original. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Next steps.

Keep a short list of known sources saved offline, including non-government humanitarian sources. Choose information based on reliability and relevance, not simply whether it is official or unofficial.
