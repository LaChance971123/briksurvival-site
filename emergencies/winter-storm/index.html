Do now.

  1. 01

    Check road conditions

    Use official closures and forecasts before deciding to travel.

  2. 02

    Keep warmth safe

    Wear dry layers; never heat a home with an oven, grill or indoor generator.

  3. 03

    Prepare for outages

    Keep essential medicines, water, food, light and charged batteries accessible.

  4. 04

    If stranded

    Call for help with your location. If running the engine briefly for heat, first clear snow from the exhaust and crack a window; monitor for carbon monoxide.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Keep dry layers, safe food, water and battery light together. If stranded in a vehicle, keep the exhaust clear before running the engine and conserve fuel; do not sleep with the engine running. Remain with the vehicle when walking would expose you to poor visibility or severe cold.

People & practical needs.

Account for children, older people, pets and anyone who needs assistance before moving. Keep shoes, medicines and a torch in the shelter area. Decide who helps whom and who carries the essential supplies; do not send a lone child to retrieve items. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: check road conditions. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Do not walk away from a stranded vehicle in poor visibility unless you know a safe nearby destination.

Keep essentials within reach

For winter storm, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: Keep dry layers, safe food, water and battery light together. If stranded in a vehicle, keep the exhaust clear before running the engine and conserve fuel; do not sleep with the engine running. Remain with the vehicle when walking would expose you to poor visibility or severe cold. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

Next steps.

Check on people who rely on powered equipment or may lack safe heat. Arrange relocation before indoor temperatures become dangerous.

First decision: Check road conditions

Use official closures and forecasts before deciding to travel. Decide which person can carry this out immediately and what would prevent it in your current location. Do not delay the protective step to complete a household worksheet.

When the original plan fails

Keep dry layers, safe food, water and battery light together. If stranded in a vehicle, keep the exhaust clear before running the engine and conserve fuel; do not sleep with the engine running. Remain with the vehicle when walking would expose you to poor visibility or severe cold. Reassess the next available location or resource using the warning signs in this guide; do not assume a promised service is operating.

Before changing location

Do not walk away from a stranded vehicle in poor visibility unless you know a safe nearby destination. Check the actual route and the needs of the person who will have the most difficulty moving. A destination is not confirmed until access and conditions have been checked.
