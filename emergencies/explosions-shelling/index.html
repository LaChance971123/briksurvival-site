Do now.

  1. 01

    Get out of exposed space

    Use a designated protective shelter if it is safely reachable. Otherwise choose the most protective intact interior space available, away from glazing. Consider fire, collapse, flooding and escape access; a basement is not safe if these hazards are present.

  2. 02

    Protect yourself from secondary hazards

    Keep away from windows, loose masonry and damaged electrical equipment. Keep footwear on when moving. Do not use a lift in a damaged building.

  3. 03

    Check people without increasing exposure

    Account for the household from shelter. Provide first aid only where you will not become another casualty. Send a brief private status message if service is available.

  4. 04

    Reassess the building

    Smoke, fire, instability or flooding may require movement to a safer reachable place. Do not interpret a quiet period as proof that streets or unexploded objects are safe.

  5. 05

    Keep debris untouched

    Never handle suspicious fragments, wires, containers or munitions. Keep children and pets away. Do not approach to take a photograph.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If broadcasts and emergency numbers fail, keep sheltering while it remains intact and safer than movement. Check essential supplies, agree on short check-ins and use trusted neighbours without sending people into exposed areas.

People & practical needs.

Assign a trusted adult to each person who needs assistance. Keep medicines, essential contacts and a workable reunion plan with the group. Make decisions around the least mobile person, not the fastest; avoid splitting the family without a clear, safe way to reconnect. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: get out of exposed space. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. No ordinary room is guaranteed protection against weapons. Do not travel through active fighting or assume a claimed safe corridor has been secured.

Keep essentials within reach

For explosions & nearby shelling, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: If broadcasts and emergency numbers fail, keep sheltering while it remains intact and safer than movement. Check essential supplies, agree on short check-ins and use trusted neighbours without sending people into exposed areas. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

Next steps.

Prepare for loss of water, power and communications. Choose a reunion contact and a second shelter option. Use recently verified humanitarian or local information to judge whether movement is safer than staying.
