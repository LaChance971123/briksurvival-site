Do-not-drink advisory

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Use commercially bottled or officially approved water for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, ice, formula and pets. Do not try to make chemically contaminated water safe by boiling. Read the notice for which other uses are allowed.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Set aside sealed water for drinking, food and medicines, including pet needs. Mark the unsafe taps so nobody uses them by habit. If you have no trusted update, keep using the safer supply; restored pressure or clear appearance does not establish safety.

People & practical needs.

Prevent accidental drinking by children, visitors or people with memory difficulties. Label restricted taps and safe containers. Arrange an alternative for medicine, oral care, pets and any medical use rather than treating those as exceptions.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Save the utility notice and alternative-water distribution location offline.

Questions in the field.

Why not simply boil it?

Chemical contamination can remain after boiling and some substances can become more concentrated. Follow the restriction exactly and obtain a verified alternate drinking source. A home treatment claim must match the identified contaminant; an ordinary filter is not a universal solution.

Can I use it for other tasks?

Use only the purposes explicitly allowed by the notice. Do not infer that cooking, brushing teeth, making ice or medicine preparation is safe because bathing is mentioned. Check the exact instruction before each household use.

Watch for.

Clear appearance and normal taste cannot confirm safety.
