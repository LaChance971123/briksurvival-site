Evacuation with access or mobility needs

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Plan movement around the person who needs the most assistance. Keep mobility aids, medicines, communication tools and essential care supplies together. Confirm accessible transport and a suitable destination directly; do not assume a shelter, lift or public rescue vehicle will be available.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Identify the actual assistance

    Write down transfer needs, walking distance, stairs, powered devices, communication needs and care routines. Ask the person what help is safe and useful.

  2. 02

    Check the route

    Assess exits and stairs before a crisis. Do not use lifts in a fire or damaged building, and do not improvise a dangerous carry.

  3. 03

    Confirm support

    Arrange more than one trusted helper and transport option. Make the responsibility specific rather than assuming someone else will come.

  4. 04

    Keep essential equipment close

    Include medicines, charger, batteries, mobility aids and a concise care note. Keep a portable backup where possible.

  5. 05

    Verify the destination

    Ask about step-free entry, toilets, charging, refrigeration, caregivers and service animals. A general promise of accessibility is not a confirmed arrangement.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use your confirmed private support network and a destination suited to care needs. If transport cannot be arranged, identify the safest available refuge and communicate the precise assistance required through a safe channel.

People & practical needs.

Practice the route using the actual mobility and communication aids. Keep chargers, essential medicines and written instructions together. Ask the person what works for them before moving them or their equipment.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Practise the plan with the person’s consent and revise it when equipment or care needs change. Keep an alternative plan for power loss and an unavailable caregiver.

Common questions.

What specific assistance is required?

Describe actions rather than only a diagnosis: stairs, transfers, communication, oxygen, charging, medication or a service animal. Make this information available to a trusted helper without publishing private medical details.

What if the planned helper cannot come?

Identify a second reachable support option and a safe waiting location suited to the hazard. Check it directly when possible. A generic shelter label does not establish accessibility, medical capability or permission to bring needed equipment.

Watch for.

Do not separate a person from essential equipment or a necessary caregiver if avoidable. An untrained lifting attempt can injure both the person and the helper.
