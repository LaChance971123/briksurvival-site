Emergency guide

Nuclear detonation

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

After a nuclear detonation, get inside the nearest sturdy building as quickly as you can. Move to a basement or central interior area, away from walls, windows, and the roof. Stay inside and tune to official updates; the first 24 hours are especially important unless the building is unsafe or authorities direct you elsewhere.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Get inside

    Do not remain outdoors looking at the blast or travel across town for a preferred shelter. A substantial building offers better protection than a vehicle or open space.

  2. 02

    Go deeper inside

    Use a basement or the middle of the building with as much material between you and outdoors as possible. Close doors and windows.

  3. 03

    If exposed outdoors, clean up

    Once sheltered, remove the outer layer of clothing and set it aside away from people; wash exposed skin with water and soap when available. This reduces contamination brought indoors.

  4. 04

    Stay tuned

    Use radio, phone, or other available channels for location-specific instructions. Avoid going out to collect family members who are already sheltered elsewhere.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Do not leave protective shelter simply because broadcasts fail. Use an intact basement or central space, keep contaminated outer clothing away from people and use covered water and food. Fire, collapse or another immediate life threat can force a move; a timer alone cannot establish safe outdoor conditions.

People & practical needs.

Keep the family together in the correct shelter for the hazard and separate potentially contaminated items from the living area. Use covered food and safe water. Prepare a bag for relocation, but do not send someone outside for it during an active release. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: get inside. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Fallout conditions vary by place and time. Do not treat a fixed distance or a clock as an all-clear; use the official instructions for your location. Potassium iodide is not a general radiation antidote and should be taken only when public-health officials advise it.

Keep essentials within reach

For nuclear detonation, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: Do not leave protective shelter simply because broadcasts fail. Use an intact basement or central space, keep contaminated outer clothing away from people and use covered water and food. Fire, collapse or another immediate life threat can force a move; a timer alone cannot establish safe outdoor conditions. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

Next steps.

Arrange water, medications, and a way to receive alerts within the shelter. Leave only for an immediate threat such as fire, collapse, or other life-threatening condition, or when officials direct a safe move.

Watch for.

Fallout conditions vary by place and time. Do not treat a fixed distance or a clock as an all-clear; use the official instructions for your location. Potassium iodide is not a general radiation antidote and should be taken only when public-health officials advise it.
