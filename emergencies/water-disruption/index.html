Emergency guide

Water disruption

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

After a water outage, damaged pipes or suspected contamination, use sealed bottled or previously stored safe water until you establish the risk. Boil-water, do-not-drink and do-not-use notices mean different things. Boiling kills germs but does not remove fuel, chemicals or radioactive contamination. If notices are unavailable, do not treat clear water or restored pressure as proof of safety.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Identify the notice

    Find the utility or health department notice for your address, its start time, reason, and permitted uses. Save a screenshot in case service fails.

  2. 02

    Match use to the instruction

    A boil-water notice generally addresses microbes; follow the authority's boil directions. If the notice says do not drink or do not use, follow those stricter limits and the specified alternative-water instructions.

  3. 03

    Protect priority needs

    Set aside approved water for drinking, infant formula, medications, tooth brushing, food preparation, and pets, as the notice directs.

  4. 04

    Wait for clearance

    Do not assume that water is safe because it looks clear or service has returned. Follow flushing or filter replacement instructions after an official all-clear.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If there is no notice after pressure loss, flooding or damaged pipes, use sealed or previously stored safe water while you establish the risk. Do not assume that boiling solves an unknown contamination problem. Label containers and keep drinking water separate from cleaning water.

People & practical needs.

Include drinking, medicines, infant feeding and food preparation in the safe-water plan. Tell every caregiver which container is clean and which tap uses are restricted. Consider people who cannot read the warning or lift a large container.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Store water and a printed copy of your utility's alert channel. Know whether anyone in the household needs extra water or cannot use a typical treatment method.

Common questions.

What does the notice actually restrict?

Read its name and affected area: boil-water, do-not-drink and do-not-use notices have different consequences. If the reason is unknown, do not assume a filter or boiling resolves it. Use a verified alternate drinking source while checking the restriction.

Someone already drank suspect water — now what?

Record the source and time and seek medical or poison guidance when reachable, especially after a chemical warning or new symptoms. Do not induce vomiting or give an improvised treatment. Severe symptoms need urgent care rather than a water-treatment tutorial.

Watch for.

Do not boil water suspected of chemical or fuel contamination. Boiling can concentrate some contaminants; use the alternative source named in the notice.
