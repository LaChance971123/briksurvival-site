Returning to a damaged home

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Do not enter a building with gas smell, floodwater, unstable walls, damaged wiring or other serious hazards. A quiet street or an opened road is not proof that a home is safe. Inspect only from a safe outside position and arrange qualified assessment for structural and utility damage. Keep children and pets away.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Check the approach

    Avoid debris, unstable trees, wires, floodwater and suspicious remnants after conflict. Do not cross a hazard to inspect a property.

  2. 02

    Look from outside

    Check for leaning walls, sagging roof, fire, gas smell and water around electrical equipment. Do not enter to locate the source of a hazard.

  3. 03

    Keep utilities untouched if unsafe

    Do not turn on damaged power, gas or flooded equipment. Qualified personnel must assess these systems; do not test them yourself.

  4. 04

    Document only when safe

    Photograph visible damage from a safe position and retain receipts. Property records are less urgent than staying out of danger.

  5. 05

    Use a temporary safe base

    Keep sleeping, food and water in safe accommodation while assessment and cleanup are arranged. Separate clean belongings from contaminated items.

Separate permission to reach the area from building safety

A road reopening or neighborhood access notice does not certify an individual home. Before travel, check whether access is permitted, the route is usable, and a safe alternative place remains available if the property cannot be entered. Take account of daylight, care needs and transport. CDC advises professional inspection before entering a damaged home; obvious damage is a reason to stay outside, not an invitation to investigate closely.

Do not bring every household member into the assessment area. Keep children, pets and people particularly vulnerable to exposure at a suitable safe base. A personal checklist cannot certify structural safety, and uncertainty is not resolved by entering for a quick look. If access or assessment cannot be obtained, continue the temporary accommodation plan rather than treating delay as clearance.

Observe from safety and stop at serious hazards

On a safe permitted approach, watch for damaged trees, fallen wires, unstable debris, floodwater and damage to surrounding structures. Keep clear of fallen or suspected live electrical lines and contaminated water. Observe the building only from a location that does not expose you to collapse or other hazards. A quiet property and absence of an obvious smell do not rule out hidden damage.

ObservationProtective decision
Obvious structural damageStay out; arrange suitable professional assessment.

Do not make a powered tool, light switch, generator, or appliance part of the initial inspection. Electrical and fuel hazards require appropriate qualified handling. If something changes while you are nearby, withdraw; photographing it is not more important than distance. Preserve a safe route back to the temporary base.

Keep utilities and backup equipment within their safety limits

CDC warns against operating electrical equipment that is wet or while standing in water. Do not energize flooded or visibly damaged systems to see whether they work. Arrange assessment through the utility or a qualified professional as appropriate. Water, drainage, gas and electricity can return at different times; one restored service does not make the others safe.

A generator does not make a damaged home habitable

Use generator-specific safety instructions and a suitable outdoor location. Never run fuel-burning equipment inside living space, a garage or another enclosed area. Do not connect a generator to damaged household wiring or improvise backfeeding.

Write which service is confirmed usable, who provided the information, and what restrictions remain. Keep an active drinking-water advisory separate from a statement that water pressure has returned. Similarly, a working outlet is not an assessment of flood-damaged wiring. Maintain an alternative for necessary care until the home can genuinely support it, rather than relying only on a restoration forecast.

Make a recovery record without risking entry

When safe, record visible damage, dates, people contacted and the next action. Keep receipts for necessary expenses and a clear private record of any professional assessment or restriction. Ask the relevant insurer or responsible organization about its process; do not assume one documentation rule applies to every policy or incident. Taking photographs must not require entering an unsafe building or handling damaged material.

Verify contractors through independently obtained information and appropriate local checks before trusting a door-to-door promise. Read the proposed scope and ask what the work does and does not cover. A cleaning quote is not structural clearance. Keep personal identity, bank details and documents away from public damage posts, and retain original records rather than relying on a social-media album.

Plan cleanup as a separate job with its own limits

Reentry, cleanup and overnight occupancy are different activities. Flood, fire, mold, sewage and structural damage require different precautions and may need specialist handling. Seek the relevant specific guidance after assessment instead of treating gloves and a dust mask as permission to tackle every material. Keep hazardous or contaminated work away from clean drinking water, food preparation and sleeping areas.

  • Define which areas are cleared for access and which remain restricted.
  • Keep children and pets away from debris, damaged materials and active cleanup.
  • Check appropriate equipment and who can safely perform the actual task.
  • Plan clean handwashing and separation of contaminated clothing and tools.
  • Do not mix cleaning products or remove structural material based on appearance.
  • Stop if a new hazard, worsening conditions or an unmet care need appears.

If cleanup is delayed, preserve a workable safe base outside the affected setting. Household fatigue, pressure to save belongings and temporary accommodation costs can push people into unsafe shortcuts. Give tasks limited achievable goals and rest, rather than making the first day an exhausting attempt to restore the whole property. Property recovery should not become another emergency.

Use a habitability review before the household returns

After appropriate professional assessment, separately check the conditions needed to live there. Safe entry for a limited purpose does not necessarily mean safe sleeping, drinking water, toilets, food storage, temperature or powered care. List the household’s essential requirements and whether each is actually available. An adult capable of a brief visit may tolerate conditions that an infant or someone requiring care cannot.

  1. Confirm the permitted use of the building and any remaining restrictions.
  2. Verify safe water, sanitation and suitable food arrangements.
  3. Check essential care, accessible movement, alarms and a usable exit.
  4. Keep the alternative destination available while unresolved needs remain.
  5. Explain changes calmly to children and other household members.
  6. Set the next review and record what would require leaving again.

Return pets under control because familiar landmarks, fences and surfaces may have changed. Keep the contact plan current if the family remains split between locations. Once immediate needs settle, review the original preparedness plan: what made return difficult, what records helped, and which reserve or contact was missing. Recovery is complete in stages; do not announce all-clear simply because one room looks normal.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Remain in the safest available temporary shelter if inspection services are not operating. Delayed assessment is not permission to occupy a visibly unsafe home.

People & practical needs.

Keep children, pets and vulnerable household members at a safe base during assessment and cleanup. Confirm essential care and habitability before returning everyone, rather than bringing the whole family for an initial inspection.

Keep essentials within reach

Current access and assessment information, a safe temporary destination, essential care supplies, private documentation equipment, verified utility and assessment contacts and a short unresolved-needs log.

Next steps.

After qualified clearance, plan protective equipment and safe cleanup for the specific hazard. Flood, fire and conflict damage require different precautions. Verify contractors before paying.

Common questions.

Does access reopening certify my home?

No. The approach, building condition, utilities and ability to live there are separate questions. Use appropriate professional assessment and keep an alternative base.

Can I test wet electrical equipment after the water drains?

Do not energize flooded or damaged equipment to test it. Arrange qualified assessment; a dry-looking surface does not establish electrical safety.

Watch for.

Do not remove load-bearing debris, enter a confined space or approach explosive debris. Personal inspection cannot certify structural safety.
