Returning to a damaged home

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Do not enter a building with gas smell, floodwater, unstable walls, damaged wiring or other serious hazards. A quiet street or an opened road is not proof that a home is safe. Inspect only from a safe outside position and arrange qualified assessment for structural and utility damage. Keep children and pets away.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Check the approach

    Avoid debris, unstable trees, wires, floodwater and suspicious remnants after conflict. Do not cross a hazard to inspect a property.

  2. 02

    Look from outside

    Check for leaning walls, sagging roof, fire, gas smell and water around electrical equipment. Do not enter to locate the source of a hazard.

  3. 03

    Keep utilities untouched if unsafe

    Do not turn on damaged power, gas or flooded equipment. Qualified personnel must assess these systems; do not test them yourself.

  4. 04

    Document only when safe

    Photograph visible damage from a safe position and retain receipts. Property records are less urgent than staying out of danger.

  5. 05

    Use a temporary safe base

    Keep sleeping, food and water in safe accommodation while assessment and cleanup are arranged. Separate clean belongings from contaminated items.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Remain in the safest available temporary shelter if inspection services are not operating. Delayed assessment is not permission to occupy a visibly unsafe home.

People & practical needs.

Keep children, pets and people vulnerable to dust or mold outside the work area. Agree who assesses access, who manages dependents and where the clean sleeping and drinking-water space will be. Returning together does not mean every person should enter at once.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

After qualified clearance, plan protective equipment and safe cleanup for the specific hazard. Flood, fire and conflict damage require different precautions. Verify contractors before paying.

Questions in the field.

Does an open road mean the building is safe?

No. Access and building safety are separate. Check structural damage, water, gas and electrical hazards from a safe position before entering. Where assessment is needed, waiting outside is safer than treating an official access reopening as a building inspection.

What should be documented first?

When safe, record damage and needed repairs before disturbing materials. Keep financial and identity records private and verify anyone offering inspection or cleanup services. Documentation must not delay escape from an immediate hazard.

Watch for.

Do not remove load-bearing debris, enter a confined space or approach explosive debris. Personal inspection cannot certify structural safety.
