Adult hands-only CPR

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

If an adult suddenly collapses, is unresponsive and is not breathing normally or is only gasping, call emergency services, get an AED and start chest compressions. Push hard and fast in the center of the chest at 100–120 compressions per minute; follow dispatcher instructions.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Check safely

    Ensure the scene is safe and check responsiveness and breathing quickly.

  2. 02

    Call and get an AED

    Use speakerphone or send someone to call and bring an AED.

  3. 03

    Give compressions

    Place both hands in the center of the chest; compress at least 2 inches, avoiding more than 2.4 inches, and allow full recoil.

  4. 04

    Continue

    Follow the AED prompts and dispatcher instructions until responders take over or normal signs of life return.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If a call will not connect, start or continue compressions. Ask another person to keep trying and bring an AED. If alone, use speakerphone while starting CPR rather than waiting for an answer. Do not leave an unresponsive person just to seek better reception.

People & practical needs.

Give someone the specific task of calling or locating care while another person provides first aid. Keep the patient warm or cool as the condition requires and record what happened and when. Do not put yourself into the same hazard or assume an ambulance has been dispatched until confirmed. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: check safely. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. This adult sudden-collapse guide does not replace infant or child CPR instructions. Gasping is not normal breathing.

For adult hands-only cpr, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: If a call will not connect, start or continue compressions. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

Next steps.

Take a CPR course. Trained rescuers can give breaths according to training; children, infants, drowning and other breathing-related arrests need appropriate CPR with breaths.
