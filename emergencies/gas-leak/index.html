Emergency guide

Natural gas leak

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

If you smell gas or suspect a leak, leave the area on foot immediately. Do not use switches, flames, phones or engines in the suspected leak area. Once safely away, call emergency services and your gas utility. Do not reenter until cleared.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Leave now

    Do not search for the leak or stop to collect belongings.

  2. 02

    Avoid ignition

    Do not turn electrical devices on or off, light flames, operate garage doors or start a vehicle nearby.

  3. 03

    Call from safety

    Give the address and what you noticed to emergency services and the utility.

  4. 04

    Keep others clear

    Wait outside the danger area and follow responder instructions.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Stay outside and away even if the utility line is busy. Keep others from entering without approaching the leak. A lack of response does not make reentry safe; use another place to stay until the hazard has been assessed by qualified personnel.

People & practical needs.

Use a short instruction everyone can follow: leave together by the nearest safe route. Include people who cannot smell well, hear an alarm or move independently. Keep the meeting point away from the suspected gas area.

Keep essentials within reach

Prioritize supplies named in this guide that are usable by the person who needs them. Keep the checklist and relevant contact details accessible. Check carrying capacity, storage and equipment instructions in advance; collecting equipment must not delay the immediate protective action.

Next steps.

Have the utility or a qualified technician restore service and inspect appliances when required.

Questions in the field.

Should I search for the source?

Leave first. Do not operate switches, phones, plugs or appliances in the suspected area and do not try to find the leak with a flame. Make the call from a safe distance after leaving.

What if someone is still inside?

Tell the emergency responder who may be inside and the last known location. Do not reenter to search or retrieve a pet. Reentry requires the appropriate qualified clearance; disappearance of the smell is not proof of safety.

Watch for.

Never use a flame to test for leaks or attempt your own repair.
