Do now.

  1. 01

    Get indoors

    Open shelters, tents and trees do not protect from lightning.

  2. 02

    Avoid conductive paths

    Inside, avoid plumbing and plugged-in equipment during lightning.

  3. 03

    Read new warnings

    Move to an interior room for severe wind or hail; use a tornado shelter if a tornado warning is issued.

  4. 04

    Wait before returning

    After the last thunder, wait at least 30 minutes before resuming outdoor activity.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use a substantial building or enclosed hard-top vehicle even when warnings cannot be checked. Avoid plumbing and wired electrical equipment during lightning. Watch for rising water and damaging wind without going outside to observe the storm.

People & practical needs.

Account for children, older people, pets and anyone who needs assistance before moving. Keep shoes, medicines and a torch in the shelter area. Decide who helps whom and who carries the essential supplies; do not send a lone child to retrieve items. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: get indoors. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Every thunderstorm can produce lightning, even without a severe thunderstorm warning.

What matters before collecting supplies?
