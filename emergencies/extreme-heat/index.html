Extreme heat

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Move to air conditioning or a designated cooling space, reduce exertion and drink water regularly unless your clinician restricts fluids. Confusion, collapse or seizures in heat can mean heat stroke: call emergency services and start rapid cooling.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Find a cooler place

    Use air conditioning, a public cooling center or another safe indoor location.

  2. 02

    Reduce heat load

    Rest, loosen unnecessary clothing and use cool wet cloths or a cool shower.

  3. 03

    Check on others

    Older adults, infants, outdoor workers and people with illness may need help. Never leave anyone or pets in a parked vehicle.

  4. 04

    Recognize an emergency

    For altered mental status or collapse, call emergency services and cool the person while waiting; do not give drinks to someone unable to swallow.

Next steps.

Plan activities for cooler hours and follow heat alerts. Arrange safe transport before the home becomes dangerously hot.

Watch for.

Heat stroke can occur with sweating. Do not wait for dry skin.
