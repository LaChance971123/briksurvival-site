Flooding

Do now ↓

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Move away from rising water to safe higher ground without crossing floodwater. Follow evacuation orders. Never walk, swim or drive through a flooded route. If trapped in a building, move to a higher level, avoid a closed attic, and call emergency services with your location.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Get out of the water’s path

    Leave low ground before routes flood. If water is already blocking your route, do not cross it.

  2. 02

    Read the current warning

    Check the affected area, issue time, instructions and road closures. A familiar road can become impassable quickly.

  3. 03

    Keep people away from hazards

    Keep children and pets out of floodwater and away from drains, culverts, electrical equipment and damaged structures.

  4. 04

    If trapped, ask for help

    Give emergency services your exact location, floor, number of people and access or medical needs. Move higher if water threatens.

Make the first decision from the water, not the map

Flooding can build slowly or arrive with little warning. It can happen while the rain is falling somewhere else. If rising water threatens you, move toward a safe higher location immediately; do not wait for an app to confirm what you can already see. Staying out of the water is the central rule.

No safe crossing depth

Do not use depth estimates, vehicle size, swimming ability or a map color to justify entering floodwater. Hidden road damage, current and electrical hazards can make appearances misleading.

Reference: National Weather Service · During a flood · Ready.gov · Floods · National Weather Service · Flood watches and warnings

For a planning check, search your home, school or workplace in FEMA’s Map Service Center, confirm the map covers the correct location, and save the map date with your notes. Ask the local floodplain administrator if the boundary or coverage is unclear. Then separately identify local evacuation instructions, higher ground and road-closure information.

A gauge is not a road inspection

A river gauge describes its measurement location; a forecast is a prediction. Neither proves that a particular street, bridge or underpass is safe. Missing data and areas without mapped flooding are not an all-clear. Never enter water because a map appears reassuring.

Outside U.S. coverage, find equivalent tools from your national weather or hydrology service and local disaster authority. An overseas map or a nearby river’s graph cannot substitute for information about your location.

Read alerts and choose an early departure trigger

What the U.S. warning language means
NoticeAction
Flood watchFlooding is possible. Check the forecast, prepare transport and supplies, and be ready to act.
Flood warningFlooding is occurring or imminent. Read the location and protective instructions and act.
Flash-flood warningRapid flooding is occurring or imminent. If in a flood-prone area, move immediately to higher ground.
Evacuation orderFollow the local order and specified route or destination. Do not wait for water to reach the doorstep.

Osprey planning worksheet: write down which warning or official instruction starts your departure, who receives it, and what happens if that person is away. Choose the higher-ground destination and a backup before rain begins. Identify the point at which a route is no longer usable; any floodwater or closure means you do not use that route.

For river flooding, keep following forecasts even after local rain stops. For sudden flash flooding, use the short response guide below. Storm surge needs a coastal evacuation plan made before the water arrives.

Prepare before the route is threatened

  • Pack food, safe water, medicines, lights, charging equipment and essential documents before an evacuation becomes urgent. Include children’s and pets’ needs.
  • Identify low spots, underpasses, stream crossings and roads that commonly flood along your usual journeys. Know a safer destination and more than one possible route.
  • Keep documents in a water-resistant container and save copies securely. Charge devices and keep a battery radio available.
  • Move valuables only while it is safe and time allows. Sandbags and property protection must never delay leaving.

Osprey practice exercise: starting from a normal evening at home, identify who finds the alert, who calls the pickup driver and who carries essentials. Walk the indoor exit route while conditions are safe. Check whether the plan still works in darkness or if the usual driver is at work. Change any step that depends on a single missing person or device.

Prepare buildings before the emergency

Ask qualified professionals about drainage, backflow prevention, pumps and building-level risk reduction. During flooding, do not enter a wet basement to save a pump or reach electrical controls. Property protection is not a reason to remain in danger.

Reference: National Weather Service · Before a flood · CDC · Electrical hazards after a disaster · Ready.gov · Floods

Make the plan usable for everyone

Flood departure plans need to reflect how people actually move and communicate. Include the person who needs assistance in the decisions. Review accessible exits, transport, medication and equipment with caregivers, building management and local emergency services before the threat develops. Plan an alternative if a helper, vehicle or normal route is unavailable.

Do not rely on an assistance registry as your only plan; responders may be delayed. If you need help now because a flood is threatening, contact emergency services and explain your location and what assistance is needed. If the danger is not immediate, use local transport or emergency-management contacts to resolve the arrangements early.

If water cuts you off, change the plan

In a building with dangerously rising water, move to the highest accessible level and call emergency services. Do not climb into a closed attic where rising water could trap you. Go onto the roof only if necessary and signal for help. Do not enter floodwater to retrieve a vehicle, equipment or belongings.

If a vehicle is trapped in rapidly moving water, Ready.gov advises staying inside; if water is rising inside, seek refuge on the roof. Contact emergency services if possible. This is an emergency fallback, not permission to drive into water or attempt a water rescue.

Osprey emergency-call prompt

“We are at [address or landmark], on [floor or vehicle location]. There are [number] people. Water is [where it is now] and [rising or unknown]. We need [mobility, medical or other assistance]. My callback number is [number].” Answer the dispatcher’s questions and follow their instructions.

If the call cannot connect, keep trying when possible, use a safe visible signal and conserve phone power. Stay with your group in the safest accessible location. Do not send someone into the water to look for a signal or improvise a rescue. No response time can be assumed.

Reference: FEMA · Shelter-in-place for flooding and flash flooding (PDF) · Ready.gov · Floods · National Weather Service · During a flood

Wait for reentry, then check hazards before cleanup

Receding water does not clear a building or a road. Wait for authorities to permit return, obey barriers and avoid unnecessary trips through affected areas. Do not enter a flood-damaged building until it has been declared safe. Water can weaken floors, walls and other structures.

Never stand in water to turn power on or off. Have the utility or a qualified electrician deal with inaccessible controls and damaged wiring. An appliance that looks dry may still need inspection. Photograph damage only from safe positions, then ask the insurer what records are needed.

Reference: National Weather Service · After a flood · CDC · Electrical hazards after a disaster · CDC · After a hurricane or tropical storm

Protect health during the recovery days

Floodwater can carry sewage, chemicals and sharp debris. Keep children and pets away. If skin contacts it, wash with soap and clean water as soon as possible. Seek medical attention for contaminated puncture wounds, embedded debris or signs of infection such as increasing redness, swelling, pain or fever.

  • Use the local drinking-water advisory for drinking, food preparation and hygiene. Do not assume the tap is safe because service has returned.
  • Discard food contaminated by floodwater and check the separate guidance for food affected by power loss. Never taste food to decide whether it is safe.
  • Keep generators outside and at least 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. Never run fuel-burning equipment in a home, garage or enclosed shelter.

Osprey recovery worksheet: list safe accommodation, drinking water, essential care, transport and a working phone contact first. Record damage and expenses separately. Decide what requires a professional and what can safely wait. Arrange someone to check in during cleanup, and stop for exhaustion, illness, new hazards or renewed warnings.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Move to safer higher ground while you have a dry route. If routes are already flooded, stay out of the water and use the safest accessible elevated location. Try emergency services, give a precise location and signal safely. Do not improvise a crossing because assistance is delayed.

People & practical needs.

Assign an adult to each child or person requesting help. Keep pets secured and access aids with their users. Count everyone at departure and arrival. A helper should not enter floodwater to collect belongings or reach another household.

Keep essentials within reach

Take only reachable essentials: medicines, phone and power bank, identification, keys, a light, water, access aids and pet supplies. Carry documents protected from water. Do not go into a basement or across water to complete the bag.

Next steps.

Keep monitoring warnings, river forecasts and local closures from your safe location. Return only when authorities permit it, and treat building, electrical, water and cleanup safety as separate checks.

My street is outside the mapped high-risk zone. Can I ignore a warning?

No. A long-term flood-risk classification does not cancel current warnings or local evacuation orders.

Water is entering the basement. Should I save appliances or switch off the power?

Do not enter water to retrieve items or reach a breaker. Keep clear and ask the utility or an electrician for safe isolation.

The rain stopped. Can I drive home?

Only when the route is open and safe and any reentry restrictions are lifted. Water and road damage can remain after rain ends.

Common questions.

Where can I look up my flood zone?

Enter your address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center linked above. This U.S. planning and insurance tool is separate from evacuation zones and current flooding.

Where can I see water levels?

Use NOAA’s National Water Prediction Service for U.S. gauge observations and forecasts. Check timestamps and coverage, and read local warnings separately. It cannot certify a safe road crossing.

Watch for.

Floodwater can arrive from upstream rain, and roads may fail beneath it. Never interpret a flood-zone map, gauge reading, navigation route or another driver’s crossing as proof that a road is safe.

Sources & context.

Civilian flood preparedness and immediate protective actions. Map and alert resources focus on U.S. coverage; elsewhere use local authorities. This is not a live flood report, rescue manual, electrical procedure or structural assessment. Official instructions and visible danger take priority.
