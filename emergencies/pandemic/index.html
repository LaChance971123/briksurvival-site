PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Create a workable sick-person area, improve ventilation where safe and separate shared food, washing and care tasks. Keep medicines and a written symptom record. Obtain disease-specific guidance when possible; do not use unproven treatments or copy isolation rules from another outbreak.

People & practical needs.

Make the care plan workable with the people, room and supplies you have. Separate clean and dirty tasks, check high-risk household members and preserve access to essential medicines. Keep a written record of symptoms and exposure rather than relying on memory. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: find current guidance. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Do not transfer isolation durations or treatment advice from one disease to another.

Keep essentials within reach

For pandemic & disease outbreak, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: Create a workable sick-person area, improve ventilation where safe and separate shared food, washing and care tasks. Keep medicines and a written symptom record. Obtain disease-specific guidance when possible; do not use unproven treatments or copy isolation rules from another outbreak. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

Next steps.

Arrange sick-leave, caregiver and household supply plans. Verify information before sharing it.

First decision: Find current guidance

Use the local health department and CDC disease-specific updates. Decide which person can carry this out immediately and what would prevent it in your current location. Do not delay the protective step to complete a household worksheet.

When the original plan fails

Create a workable sick-person area, improve ventilation where safe and separate shared food, washing and care tasks. Keep medicines and a written symptom record. Obtain disease-specific guidance when possible; do not use unproven treatments or copy isolation rules from another outbreak. Reassess the next available location or resource using the warning signs in this guide; do not assume a promised service is operating.

Before changing location

Do not transfer isolation durations or treatment advice from one disease to another. Check the actual route and the needs of the person who will have the most difficulty moving. A destination is not confirmed until access and conditions have been checked.
