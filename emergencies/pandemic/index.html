Do now.

  1. 01

    Find current guidance

    Use the local health department and CDC disease-specific updates.

  2. 02

    Reduce transmission

    Improve cleaner indoor air and use recommended precautions for the way the disease spreads.

  3. 03

    Plan care

    Ask your clinician about risk, treatment access and continuity of essential medicines.

  4. 04

    Know urgent signs

    Seek emergency help for severe breathing difficulty, new confusion or other life-threatening symptoms.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Create a workable sick-person area, improve ventilation where safe and separate shared food, washing and care tasks. Keep medicines and a written symptom record. Obtain disease-specific guidance when possible; do not use unproven treatments or copy isolation rules from another outbreak.

People & practical needs.

Make the care plan workable with the people, room and supplies you have. Separate clean and dirty tasks, check high-risk household members and preserve access to essential medicines. Keep a written record of symptoms and exposure rather than relying on memory.

Keep essentials within reach

Keep essential medicines, drinking water, a battery light, shoes, phone power and a written contact card within reach. Adapt the load to the hazard and the people with you; do not delay escape to collect equipment.

Next steps.

Arrange sick-leave, caregiver and household supply plans. Verify information before sharing it.

Watch for.

Do not transfer isolation durations or treatment advice from one disease to another.
