Rip currents: escape the pull, help from shore

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Do not swim directly against the current. Float to conserve energy; if you can swim, move parallel to shore to leave the current, then return toward the beach. Wave and call for help. A bystander should alert a lifeguard or emergency services and throw flotation rather than enter the water.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Conserve your effort

    Avoid exhausting yourself by fighting toward shore against the pull. Float or tread water if you cannot swim out.

  2. 02

    Signal clearly

    Wave and shout so someone on shore knows you need help.

  3. 03

    Help without becoming another casualty

    Get a lifeguard, call emergency services when possible, and throw something that floats. Do not form a human chain.

  4. 04

    Prevent another exposure

    Check beach warnings and use guarded swimming areas. Calm skies and small waves do not establish safe water.

Before your beach visit

Editorial family plan: name the guarded swimming area, read the posted warnings, identify the nearest beach access marker and decide who supervises each child. Write the access location in a message before swimming so a bystander can tell responders where you are.

Give responders a usable location

From shore, describe the beach name, nearest numbered access or lifeguard tower, number of people in difficulty and visible landmarks. Keep watching the person so you can indicate their position; send another adult to meet responders if available.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If no lifeguard is visible, shout for someone to contact emergency services while you look for flotation to throw from a safe position. An untrained swimming rescue can create a second emergency.

People & practical needs.

Agree on direct adult supervision and a meeting place outside the water. A flotation aid is not a substitute for supervision. If conditions are uncertain, choose a shore activity rather than entering the surf.

Keep essentials within reach

Beach location, phone kept dry, local warning information and supervision arrangements. Do not delay alerting help to retrieve supplies.

Next steps.

After a rescue, seek qualified assessment for breathing difficulty or other symptoms. Reassess the whole group before anyone returns to the water.

Unable to swim free?

Stop fighting the current, float and signal.

Someone else is caught?

Alert help and throw flotation from shore.

Common questions.

Does a sunny day mean it is safe?

No. Read the beach forecast and flags and ask the lifeguard about conditions. If in doubt, stay out.

Watch for.

Do not assume a gap between breaking waves is a safe entry route. Avoid piers and jetties where currents can concentrate.
