Heating failure in cold weather

Do now.

  1. 01

    Get dry and insulated

    Replace wet clothes, layer loose dry clothing and insulate beneath the body as well as above it. Keep vulnerable people away from draughts.

  2. 02

    Use one workable space

    Choose a dry room that remains safe and has a usable exit. Close unnecessary interior doors without blocking ventilation needed by approved heating appliances.

  3. 03

    Avoid combustion shortcuts

    Use only heating equipment approved for indoor use according to its instructions, with working CO alarms. A grill, oven or indoor generator is not a backup heater.

  4. 04

    Check people regularly

    Cold can impair judgement. Pay particular attention to infants, older people and anyone with illness or limited mobility. Do not rely on a person saying they feel fine.

  5. 05

    Move before severe exposure

    Confirm a warmer destination and safe route while people can still travel. Do not cross dangerous ice or an active hazard merely to reach a public warming centre.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use the safest dry shelter and insulation available while confirming a warmer private or community destination. Leave early if you cannot keep vulnerable people warm rather than waiting for official shelter.

People & practical needs.

Assign a trusted adult to each person who needs assistance. Keep medicines, essential contacts and a workable reunion plan with the group. Make decisions around the least mobile person, not the fastest; avoid splitting the family without a clear, safe way to reconnect. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: get dry and insulated. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Do not give alcohol as a warming method or rub frostbitten skin. Fuel-burning devices can create lethal CO even when you cannot smell anything.
