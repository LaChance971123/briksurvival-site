Evacuating with pets

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Take pets when you can evacuate safely, using a carrier or lead and a destination that accepts them. Keep food, water, medicines, identification and veterinary information together. Do not delay escape into immediate danger or reenter a hazardous building for an animal.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Contain animals early

    Use a secure carrier or lead before loading or opening doors. Distress and noise can cause animals to run.

  2. 02

    Confirm the destination

    Ask whether pets are accepted and what documents or carriers are needed. Make a backup arrangement with a trusted person.

  3. 03

    Pack basic care

    Take normal food, safe water, medicines, waste supplies and a familiar blanket if readily available. Include pet water in the household total.

  4. 04

    Keep identification current

    Use a tag and a recent photo, and check microchip contact details before emergencies. Keep veterinary records privately.

  5. 05

    Avoid unsafe rescue

    Tell reachable responders where an animal may be, but do not return to fire, floodwater or active danger yourself.

Make a separate departure task for every animal

Name who collects each animal, which carrier or restraint is used, and where the animal will travel. Include pets that hide, cannot be handled by every adult, or need special care. Start containment early in a developing evacuation when it is safe; searching for a frightened cat after doors open can consume the departure time. Keep the human immediate-escape plan separate from planned packing.

Editorial example: two dogs have bags of food ready but only one adult can safely handle both leads while helping a child. The gap is responsibility and transport, not food. Practice a calm loading sequence in normal conditions and adjust the plan before warning time is short. Do not delay escape from a fire or reenter danger to collect equipment or an animal.

Reference: American Red Cross · Pet disaster preparedness

Confirm admission rather than assuming a shelter accepts pets

Check the destination directly before relying on it. Ask whether it accepts the animal’s species, size and number, what containment and records are required, whether animals stay with owners, and what care remains the owner’s responsibility. A hotel, host, boarding facility or evacuation center may have different rules and limited space. Keep a realistic alternative that is not exposed to exactly the same hazard.

Do not conflate pets and service animals. They may have different accommodation rules. Red Cross guidance describes possible separate animal housing depending on circumstances and welcomes service animals that assist people with disabilities. Use the specific facility’s current arrangements; do not present an old shelter list or general policy as a confirmed space for tonight.

Pack usable care, identification and a concise instruction card

Use the normal suitable food, safe water, appropriate bowls, prescribed medicines and needed waste supplies. Count pet water and food separately so human reserves are not silently assigned twice. Include the equipment needed to open or serve the actual food. Ask the veterinarian about special diets, medicine storage and emergency care needs; do not improvise a dose or sedate an animal from a generic evacuation tip.

  • Keep a suitable carrier, harness or lead with an accessible loading location.
  • Use a current identification tag and review microchip contact details where applicable.
  • Keep a recent photograph and veterinary information privately with the responsible adult.
  • Write the feeding routine, prescribed instructions, veterinarian contact and important handling needs.
  • Include appropriate waste containment and clean-up supplies without packing chemicals beside food.
  • Add a familiar item if manageable, while keeping essential human and animal care accessible.

Label by animal where needs differ. A generic bag saying pets can conceal a missing medicine or the wrong food. Keep a grab list for daily-use care items and review it after treatment changes. Store records so they can be found even if a phone fails, while protecting information that is unnecessary for admission or care.

Reference: American Red Cross · Pet disaster preparedness

Test transport space and controlled loading

Check the actual vehicle with people, mobility equipment, bags and suitable animal restraint. Carrier dimensions and access matter as much as total cargo space. Use the restraint and transport equipment as intended and maintain appropriate ventilation and temperature. Never leave an animal in a hot vehicle while negotiating admission or checking a room. Travel conditions and the animal’s health may require veterinary advice.

Keep doors and animals controlled during transfers. Do not let children manage a distressed animal beyond their ability. If the usual adult is away, identify a trusted helper with appropriate access and knowledge before the emergency, rather than counting on an unfamiliar volunteer. A helper must also have a safe route; do not ask them to enter active fire, floodwater or another dangerous area.

Maintain care in temporary accommodation

On arrival, count animals and confirm that each is secured in the intended arrangement. Place food, medicines and clean equipment where the responsible person can find them. Keep animals under appropriate control around unfamiliar people and other animals. Distress can change behavior, so do not assume a normally friendly animal is comfortable with crowding, noise or strangers handling it.

Keep a daily care record if responsibility changes

Record feeding, prescribed care, waste needs and any concern for the next trusted handler. Note what was actually done rather than leaving two adults to guess whether medicine was already given.

Check temperature, available water and suitable rest without relying only on the weather outdoors. Seek veterinary help for worrying changes or injury. Maintain hygiene between animal waste, meal tasks and child care. If separate boarding is necessary, verify who holds the animal, how updates are obtained, and how collection will work. Keep the information with the household coordinator so onward movement does not leave the animal unaccounted for.

Plan lost-pet checks and safe return

If an animal is missing, record its last confirmed place and time, description, identification and photograph. Contact verified animal services, veterinary contacts or the relevant facility through independently obtained channels when available. Do not enter a dangerous area or pay an unknown caller solely on a claim of finding the animal. Keep public appeals limited to useful information and protect private identity and location details.

  1. Account for each animal before every move and after arrival.
  2. Tell the trusted handler or household coordinator of any missing animal.
  3. Record sightings separately from confirmed identification.
  4. Verify found-animal reports through a known service or safe trusted contact.
  5. Confirm collection and update outdated notices when reunited.
  6. Return home only when the setting is suitable for people and animals. Keep animals controlled around damage and debris.

After an evacuation, review the part that actually failed: capture, loading, admission, care or communication. Replace used supplies and adjust responsibility before the next event. Livestock, large numbers of animals and specialist species need their own transport and welfare plan; this household guide does not make a small pet carrier plan sufficient for them.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Use a confirmed trusted host or animal-care arrangement and keep a viable alternative. Ask the actual evacuation site about current animal housing rather than assuming every site accepts pets or keeps them beside their owners.

People & practical needs.

Assign a capable handler to each animal while another adult manages children or access needs. Test transport with the actual people and equipment, and verify pet and service-animal arrangements separately.

Keep essentials within reach

Suitable carriers or restraints, normal food and safe water, bowls and required opener, prescribed medicines, waste supplies, current identification, private veterinary records and confirmed destination details.

Next steps.

Check animals regularly for overheating, cold and distress in transport. Keep them restrained in crowded temporary accommodation and maintain hygiene.

Common questions.

Are pets accepted wherever people shelter?

Do not assume so. Verify the particular site’s current admission, containment and care arrangements, including any separate housing. Keep an alternative suitable destination.

What if a pet is still inside a dangerous building?

Do not reenter active danger. Give reachable responders accurate information from safety and use verified animal-assistance channels when available.

Watch for.

A service animal and a pet may have different accommodation rules. Never leave animals in a hot vehicle while arranging shelter.
