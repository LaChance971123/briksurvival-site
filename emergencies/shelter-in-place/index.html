Shelter-in-place

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Get inside if the outdoor hazard makes travel more dangerous, and choose the room for that hazard: a lowest-floor sturdy interior area for a tornado, protective interior mass for fallout, or few outside openings for an airborne chemical release. Close openings and control ventilation only as the hazard requires. Do not remain in a burning, flooding or collapsing building, and do not wait for a message when immediate danger is visible.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Identify the hazard

    Read the actual shelter instruction and affected area.

  2. 02

    Choose the correct room

    Use an interior low room for tornadoes, a basement or central area for fallout, and the room specified for a chemical event.

  3. 03

    Bring immediate essentials

    Take people, pets, medicines, phone power and a radio without delaying shelter.

  4. 04

    Stay informed

    Remain until officials advise otherwise, unless fire or another immediate danger makes the building unsafe.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

Choose the room for the hazard, not the phrase: lowest sturdy interior area for tornadoes, protective interior mass for fallout, and a room with few outside openings for an airborne chemical threat. Leave a shelter that is burning, flooding or collapsing rather than following a generic stay-inside rule.

People & practical needs.

Keep the group together with a named adult responsible for each child or person needing assistance. Count people at every departure and arrival. Write contact details on paper, retain necessary medicines and agree what to do if one person cannot travel. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: identify the hazard. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Do not apply chemical room-sealing instructions to every hazard or remain in a burning building.

Keep essentials within reach

For shelter-in-place, prioritize only items needed for the first action and the fallback: Choose the room for the hazard, not the phrase: lowest sturdy interior area for tornadoes, protective interior mass for fallout, and a room with few outside openings for an airborne chemical threat. Leave a shelter that is burning, flooding or collapsing rather than following a generic stay-inside rule. Keep the relevant checklist and contact information within reach. Equipment retrieval must not delay the protective step.

Next steps.

Plan safe rooms for the hazards in your area and keep an alert source that works during outages.
