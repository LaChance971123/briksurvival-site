Do now.

  1. 01

    Decide whether travel is necessary

    Compare the danger of staying with the risks of the route. Do not travel only to investigate a closure. Have a known destination and another option.

  2. 02

    Verify current conditions

    Compare recent trusted local information with independently verified humanitarian information. Check dates and exact locations; old route advice may be unsafe.

  3. 03

    Keep essential documents accessible

    Carry identification and medical information securely. Know where they are so you do not need a hurried search. Do not display sensitive details unnecessarily.

  4. 04

    Avoid escalating an encounter

    Remain calm, avoid sudden gestures and do not film an encounter when doing so increases danger. There is no universal script that guarantees safe treatment.

  5. 05

    Keep the household together

    Have an agreed check-in and separation plan. Do not send a child or vulnerable person ahead alone to test a route.

If help is unavailable.

If nobody can verify a route and travel is not immediately necessary, remain in the safer available place while reassessing. Do not assume a public convoy or unofficial driver has secured protection.

People & practical needs.

Assign a trusted adult to each person who needs assistance. Keep medicines, essential contacts and a workable reunion plan with the group. Make decisions around the least mobile person, not the fastest; avoid splitting the family without a clear, safe way to reconnect. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: decide whether travel is necessary. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens.
