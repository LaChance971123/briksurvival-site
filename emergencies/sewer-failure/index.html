Sewer failure & toilets without service

Do now ↓

QUICK ANSWER · START HERE

Stop using plumbing that is backing up or may discharge sewage into living areas. Keep children and pets away from contaminated water. Separate toilet waste from drinking water, food and washing areas, and use a contained temporary toilet arrangement until safe disposal and service are established. Do not flush blindly during a sewer disruption.

Do now.

  1. 01

    Check for backup

    If drains or toilets back up, stop adding water and keep clear of the affected area. Do not operate electrical equipment in standing water.

  2. 02

    Keep clean supplies separate

    Protect drinking-water containers and food. Use a designated clean handwashing area with safe water and soap.

  3. 03

    Use a contained temporary option

    Use a purpose-made portable or camping toilet according to instructions, or a sturdy lidded container with appropriate leak-resistant liners as a temporary arrangement. Keep it stable and away from children.

  4. 04

    Handle waste cautiously

    Wear gloves when available, close liners securely and prevent leaks. Wash hands after handling and before food preparation. Keep waste away from water sources.

  5. 05

    Confirm disposal

    Use an established safe disposal service or locally appropriate sanitation guidance. Do not dump waste into streets, rivers, storm drains or open ground.

Septic systems after flooding

Water supply and wastewater service are different

A toilet that has no flushing water is not the same as a toilet connected to a damaged or backed-up sewer. Identify whether the problem is water supply, drainage, the building’s internal plumbing, a septic system, or a wider wastewater failure. Do not keep pouring water into a toilet to test it when sewage may back up or service is restricted.

Check utility or property-management information when reachable and record the affected systems and restrictions. In a shared building, your sink may connect to a wider problem. A neighbor’s toilet working once does not establish that the whole drainage system is safe. Significant sewage contamination requires qualified assessment and cleanup.

Set a boundary around contamination

Keep children, pets, food, and clean water out of affected areas. Avoid touching or walking through sewage, especially where electrical equipment or building damage may also be involved. Do not enter standing water to operate electrical switches or appliances. A bleach solution does not make a sewage flood safe to enter.

For a small household, draw the actual room layout and identify where each function can take place. If there is no way to keep living and care areas clear of contamination, staying may not be workable. Do not hide that problem behind an inventory showing plenty of food and batteries.

Choose a temporary toilet for the user and setting

A purpose-made portable toilet can provide a contained option when used according to its instructions. Check the seat stability, user capacity, cleaning requirements, liners or chemicals specified by the product, and the actual disposal route. A portable toilet is not complete simply because someone can sit on it.

  • Choose a stable, accessible position with appropriate privacy and lighting; keep it away from food and drinking-water storage.
  • Check how a person with limited mobility transfers, reaches supplies, and receives assistance. Do not improvise an unstable seat or unsafe lifting routine.
  • Keep required consumables and instructions together. Different toilet systems may use different waste-handling arrangements.
  • Keep the setup secured from children and pets, with a responsible adult managing used materials.
  • Use local public-health sanitation guidance for improvised alternatives and disposal. Do not assume another state’s emergency method is permitted in your location.

If an improvised contained arrangement is the only immediate option, it still needs stability, appropriate leak-resistant containment, privacy, handwashing, and a plan for secure temporary holding. This guide does not provide a universal construction or waste-treatment recipe. Where those conditions cannot be met, prioritize a safer sanitation location.

Make waste handling and handwashing one task

Prepare the handwashing area before handling waste. Use suitable water and soap, and arrange runoff without relying on failed drains. CDC recommends washing hands for at least 20 seconds. Gloves can reduce contact during handling but do not replace handwashing or remove the need to keep clean supplies separate.

  1. Prepare needed containment and cleaning supplies without placing them in the food area.
  2. Follow the toilet’s actual procedure and local sanitation instructions for closing and handling used materials.
  3. Prevent leaks and keep the closed waste container protected from access.
  4. Clean the appropriate surfaces with suitable products according to their instructions; never mix chemicals.
  5. Remove used gloves appropriately, then wash hands before returning to food or care tasks.

Assign the dirty task to a capable adult where possible, and keep the routine simple enough that another adult can understand it. The person assisting a dependent household member may need separate help to handle waste. Do not rely on a child to recognize contamination or carry a heavy used container.

Reference: CDC · Emergency personal hygiene

A disposal plan is essential

Confirm what the local wastewater utility, public-health service, or emergency collection arrangement accepts and where. Keep the instructions, opening status, transport requirement, and contact details. Rules for human waste, portable-toilet chemicals, diapers, and ordinary rubbish may differ. An ordinary collection vehicle passing the street does not authorize every waste type.

Do not discharge waste into streets, storm drains, rivers, or open ground. Do not invent a burial or chemical-treatment solution without appropriate local guidance. If collection is unavailable, maintain secure contained holding while seeking a verified option. If the dwelling cannot sustain it safely, reconsider remaining there.

Restart service and review what failed

Water pressure returning does not prove that the sewer or septic system is ready. Confirm the applicable instructions before resuming use. Arrange qualified assessment where there was damage or significant contamination. A normal-looking drain should not override a continuing service restriction.

  1. Confirm the status of wastewater service and the building’s affected plumbing.
  2. Keep contaminated materials and cleanup tasks separated from normal living and meal preparation.
  3. Follow the applicable cleanup and disposal instructions; use qualified help for significant contamination.
  4. Replenish toilet, handwashing, and personal-care consumables.
  5. Record the usable capacity and limitations of the temporary arrangement.
  6. Update the relocation trigger if the household could not maintain safe sanitation.

For a preparedness exercise, inspect the unused toilet equipment, read its instructions, find suitable storage locations, and identify the normal and emergency disposal contacts. Do not deliberately disable household plumbing or generate waste for practice. Test that the intended user can use the position and supplies safely, with normal facilities available.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If no collection is operating, contain waste securely in a protected place away from living, food and water areas while seeking a verified disposal option. Do not improvise an open discharge that endangers neighbours.

People & practical needs.

Choose a stable accessible toilet position, appropriate privacy and assistance, and a safe handwashing arrangement. Plan diapers, continence care and soiled-clothing containment explicitly. Keep children and pets away from waste and contaminated areas; share cleanup responsibilities only with capable adults.

Keep essentials within reach

Suitable stable toilet equipment and its instructions, required consumables, safe handwashing water and soap, appropriate containment, a light and verified disposal contacts. Keep clean and dirty supplies separate.

Next steps.

When water returns, sewer service may still be damaged. Resume plumbing only once its use and disposal route are safe; arrange qualified cleanup for significant sewage contamination.

Common questions.

Can I flush if I have a bucket of water?

Only if the plumbing and wastewater system are usable and there is no relevant restriction. Extra water can worsen backup when drainage has failed. Establish the system status before adding it.

Can contained human waste go in ordinary rubbish?

Do not assume so. Check what the actual local collection or disposal arrangement accepts, including the container and product requirements. Secure containment buys time but does not create a legal or safe disposal route.

Watch for.

Bleach does not make sewage floodwater safe to enter. Never mix cleaning chemicals. Contamination and infection risks are greater for children and immunocompromised people.
