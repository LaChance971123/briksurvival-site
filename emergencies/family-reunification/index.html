Family separation & reunification

Do now.

  1. 01

    Record facts

    Write the last confirmed contact, location, time, description and medical needs. Distinguish what you know from rumours. Keep a recent photo privately if available.

  2. 02

    Try agreed channels

    Send a short text to the person and the out-of-area contact. Leave your own location and next check-in time. A sent message may not be delivered.

  3. 03

    Use the safe meeting plan

    Go to the meeting point only if the route and location are safe. Do not send several people independently into a hazardous area.

  4. 04

    Verify tracing services

    Use independently found Red Cross or humanitarian contacts. Check the organisation rather than trusting a forwarded number, and ask how information will be protected.

  5. 05

    Protect children

    Never hand a child to an unverified adult promising reunification. Keep a written contact card and involve a trusted caregiver or verified protection service.

Stabilize your own group and record the last confirmed facts

Before searching for anyone, protect the people already with you and establish a safe contactable base. A frantic series of moves can create another missing person and erase the location someone expects you to use. If a child is missing or someone may be in immediate danger, contact the appropriate emergency or protection service through a safe available channel. Do not postpone that request while completing a perfect record.

Separate what you witnessed from a reported sighting and a rumor. “Someone said they were at a shelter” is not a confirmed arrival. Retain the original message if safe, note its time, and check the sender through a known channel. Keep the working record private rather than placing a full identity profile on social media.

Use the existing reunion plan before inventing several searches

Try the person’s agreed contact channel and the household’s outside coordinator. Send one short factual update with your location, the people with you, and a next check-in time. Ask for confirmation when practical. If a prearranged meeting place is reachable and appropriate to the hazard, use the written plan. Do not treat that plan as permission to cross danger or enter a damaged building.

A useful division of work is for one adult to contact the known school or host while another stays with children and maintains the record. Give each task a clear end point and reporting time. Avoid several people independently driving to the same unverified location. If no message gets through, maintain a predictable safe location as far as the incident allows.

Use school, childcare and caregiver release procedures

A school or care facility may move people to a different site and may restrict collection to authorized adults. Before an incident, ask for its emergency contact, release, relocation and identification procedure. Put the relevant details with the household plan. During an incident, use independently verified facility information rather than an address supplied by an unknown caller claiming to have your child.

  • Name the authorized backup collector and make sure the facility’s records agree.
  • Record what identification or permission the actual facility requires, without assuming one universal rule.
  • Arrange who stays with other children while a collector travels.
  • Use a calm handover: verify the child’s identity, the collecting adult and the confirmed destination.
  • If someone offers to move or reunite a child, verify their role and organization before allowing a handover; seek verified protection help when needed.

For an adult requiring assistance, record how they communicate and who is authorized to help. A caregiver’s failure to answer does not prove that the adult was transferred elsewhere. Check the facility or known support contact and ask for a specific confirmed status. Respect privacy limits; an institution may need to verify your identity before disclosing information.

Find an appropriate tracing or welfare service and verify it

The American Red Cross describes different family and welfare inquiry services for U.S. disaster separations, with eligibility criteria. Use its current contact-and-locate page or an independently verified local chapter to learn which service fits. For international separation, use verified Red Cross or Red Crescent family-links channels appropriate to the situation. Neither an inquiry submission nor a registration guarantees a rapid location or reunion.

Check reported sightings without exposing the person

A report should include when and where the person was seen, what the observer actually knows, and a safe way to verify it. Several reposts of one claim do not create several independent sightings. Use known contacts or the relevant verified service to check the report. Do not send a child, a vulnerable adult, or an untrained volunteer into a hazardous search area.

Public appeals need a privacy decision

Consider what minimum information could help, who might see it, and whether the person faces a safety threat. Keep full identity numbers, medical records, private addresses and live movements out of public posts. Ask a verified tracing service what publication is appropriate.

Editorial example: a stranger says a relative is at a hotel and asks for a fee before giving a room number. Record the claim, independently contact the hotel or verified service through a known number where appropriate, and do not pay merely to unlock the story. A convincing photograph can be old or copied. Avoid promising a reward or sharing more private information before verification.

Confirm the reunion and close the information loop

When contact is restored, confirm identity through familiar knowledge or a trusted verified handover, establish the person’s actual location and condition, and agree how to reconnect safely. Someone answering a borrowed phone may be reachable but still unable to travel. Do not call the search finished until the coordinator understands what support or onward arrangement is needed.

  1. Confirm who was reached, where they are and when the contact happened.
  2. Check whether urgent care, an accessible journey or a trusted caregiver is needed.
  3. Tell the household coordinator and relevant verified tracing services.
  4. Update or remove outdated public requests where you control them.
  5. Keep necessary case and care records private, and stop unnecessary circulation.
  6. Review what caused separation and improve contact cards or release arrangements.

Reunion can be physically and emotionally difficult. Provide calm information, suitable food and water, essential care, and rest where available. Avoid requiring a child to repeatedly recount frightening details to every adult. Once safe, replace assumptions in the plan with what actually worked: a reachable contact, a clear collector, a confirmed destination, and a paper fallback.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If networks fail, follow the written reunion plan only where safe and leave a message with the agreed trusted contact. Keep your own location predictable rather than repeatedly moving between unsafe places.

People & practical needs.

Keep children with a trusted responsible adult and assign one person to coordinate confirmed information. Plan communication, authorized collection and care needs without sending several people into unsafe searches.

Keep essentials within reach

A private last-contact record, current photograph where available, household contact card, school or caregiver release details, a working communication method and verified case references.

Next steps.

Keep one person coordinating the information so sightings, contacts and dates are not lost. Update the record when someone is found and tell verified tracing services to stop circulating old requests.

Common questions.

Should I post all identity details publicly?

No. Limit any appeal to useful appropriate information and seek verified tracing guidance. Keep identity numbers, private medical records, addresses and live movements out of public posts.

What closes the search?

Confirm identity, location and any assistance needed, tell the coordinator and relevant tracing services, and update obsolete requests. A reported sighting alone is not a verified reunion.

Watch for.

Scammers may demand money or identity documents. Do not post full identity numbers, sensitive addresses or real-time movements publicly.
