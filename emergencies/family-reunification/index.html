Do now.

  1. 01

    Record facts

    Write the last confirmed contact, location, time, description and medical needs. Distinguish what you know from rumours. Keep a recent photo privately if available.

  2. 02

    Try agreed channels

    Send a short text to the person and the out-of-area contact. Leave your own location and next check-in time. A sent message may not be delivered.

  3. 03

    Use the safe meeting plan

    Go to the meeting point only if the route and location are safe. Do not send several people independently into a hazardous area.

  4. 04

    Verify tracing services

    Use independently found Red Cross or humanitarian contacts. Check the organisation rather than trusting a forwarded number, and ask how information will be protected.

  5. 05

    Protect children

    Never hand a child to an unverified adult promising reunification. Keep a written contact card and involve a trusted caregiver or verified protection service.

PLAN AROUND WHAT YOU HAVE

If help is unavailable.

If networks fail, follow the written reunion plan only where safe and leave a message with the agreed trusted contact. Keep your own location predictable rather than repeatedly moving between unsafe places.

People & practical needs.

Assign a trusted adult to each person who needs assistance. Keep medicines, essential contacts and a workable reunion plan with the group. Make decisions around the least mobile person, not the fastest; avoid splitting the family without a clear, safe way to reconnect. For this situation, brief every person on the first action: record facts. Agree who will manage children, pets or mobility needs while that action happens. Scammers may demand money or identity documents. Do not post full identity numbers, sensitive addresses or real-time movements publicly.

Next steps.

Keep one person coordinating the information so sightings, contacts and dates are not lost. Update the record when someone is found and tell verified tracing services to stop circulating old requests.

First decision: Record facts

Write the last confirmed contact, location, time, description and medical needs. Distinguish what you know from rumours. Keep a recent photo privately if available. Decide which person can carry this out immediately and what would prevent it in your current location. Do not delay the protective step to complete a household worksheet.

When the original plan fails

If networks fail, follow the written reunion plan only where safe and leave a message with the agreed trusted contact. Keep your own location predictable rather than repeatedly moving between unsafe places. Reassess the next available location or resource using the warning signs in this guide; do not assume a promised service is operating.

Before changing location

Scammers may demand money or identity documents. Do not post full identity numbers, sensitive addresses or real-time movements publicly. Check the actual route and the needs of the person who will have the most difficulty moving. A destination is not confirmed until access and conditions have been checked.
